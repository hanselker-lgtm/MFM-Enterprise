# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the ninety-fourth document in the MFM v1.2 Steady-State series.

It follows MFM v1.2-Steady-State-93 – Enterprise IT Operations, Monitoring, Event Management, Job Scheduling, Batch Processing, Operations Control & Operational Assurance.

The purpose is to establish the permanent enterprise operating model for change governance, change strategy, change ownership, change authority, standard changes, normal changes, emergency changes, change intake, change classification, change assessment, risk and impact assessment, change approval, change scheduling, change collision management, release planning, release packaging, deployment governance, implementation, validation, rollback, change communications, post-implementation review, change failure analysis, change metrics, dashboards, change assurance, findings, exceptions, remediation, maturity and continual enterprise change management improvement.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must enable controlled and traceable change while protecting service availability, security, resilience, compliance and business outcomes.**

# 3. Change Governance Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Establish clear authority, accountability and control over changes to enterprise services, technology, processes and configurations.**

# 4. Change Strategy Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Align change activity with business priorities, service requirements, risk appetite, architecture, security, compliance and operational capacity.**

# 5. Release Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Coordinate related changes into controlled releases that can be planned, tested, deployed and validated as coherent service outcomes.**

# 6. Deployment Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Deploy approved changes through repeatable, controlled and recoverable implementation processes.**

# 7. Change Assurance Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Provide reliable evidence that material changes were properly assessed, authorized, implemented, validated and reviewed.**

# 98. Definition of Ready

A change, release, deployment, emergency change, exception, remediation or assurance review is Ready when scope, owner, affected services, classification, risk, impact, dependencies, implementation approach, testing, rollback or recovery, approvals, communication and acceptance criteria are defined.

# 100. Final Change Governance Principle

> **MFM must enable controlled and traceable change while protecting service availability, security, resilience, compliance and business outcomes.**

# 101. Final Risk Principle

> **Change risk and impact must be assessed before material changes are authorized, with controls proportionate to complexity, criticality and reversibility.**

# 102. Final Approval Principle

> **Material changes must be approved by appropriately authorized decision-makers before implementation unless defined emergency procedures apply.**

# 103. Final Testing Principle

> **Changes must be tested according to risk and impact, with sufficient evidence to demonstrate readiness for implementation.**

# 104. Final Release Principle

> **Related changes should be coordinated through controlled release management to reduce deployment risk and improve service outcomes.**

# 105. Final Deployment Principle

> **Deployments must use approved procedures, controlled access, defined validation and appropriate rollback or recovery arrangements.**

# 106. Final Emergency Principle

> **Emergency changes must remain controlled even when rapid action is required, with appropriate authority and retrospective review.**

# 107. Final Evidence Principle

> **Material change decisions and implementations must be supported by reliable, complete and traceable evidence.**

# 108. Final Assurance Principle

> **Change and release governance must be periodically assessed through risk-based assurance and measurable control effectiveness.**

# 109. Final Improvement Principle

> **Change failures, incidents, rollbacks, post-implementation findings and performance trends must continuously improve MFM's change capability.**

# 110. Final Integration Principle

> **Change Management must integrate with Service Management, IT Operations, Release Management, Configuration Management, Asset Management, Enterprise Architecture, Security, Identity, Data, Privacy, Finance, Procurement, Suppliers, Risk, Compliance, Legal, Projects and Business Continuity.**
