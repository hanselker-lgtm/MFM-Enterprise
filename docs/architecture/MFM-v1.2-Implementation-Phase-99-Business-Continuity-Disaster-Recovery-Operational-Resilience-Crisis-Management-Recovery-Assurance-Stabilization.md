# 1. Purpose

This document defines the ninety-ninth implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-98 – Third-Party, Supplier, Procurement, Contract, Vendor Risk, Service Provider & Supply-Chain Assurance Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish a controlled business continuity, disaster recovery, operational resilience, crisis management and recovery assurance capability covering business continuity governance, business impact analysis, critical business services, recovery objectives, continuity strategies, disaster recovery, recovery plans, crisis management, crisis communications, emergency decision-making, dependency mapping, recovery testing, exercises, backup and restore assurance, operational resilience, supplier continuity and recovery assurance.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must maintain the capability to continue critical operations during disruption, respond to crises in a coordinated manner, recover prioritized services within defined objectives and demonstrate through testing and evidence that recovery arrangements remain effective.**

# 129. Definition of Ready

A resilience work item is Ready when:

- The critical service or capability is identified.
- Business impact and criticality are understood.
- Recovery objectives are defined or ready for assessment.
- Dependencies are identified.
- Required resources and owners are known.
- Testing and evidence requirements are understood.
- Relevant supplier, security, privacy and operational constraints are identified.

# 131. Final Continuity Principle

> **Critical services must have defined continuity arrangements proportionate to their impact and recovery requirements.**

---

# 132. Final Recovery Principle

> **Recovery objectives must be explicit, measurable and validated through appropriate recovery arrangements and testing.**

---

# 133. Final Dependency Principle

> **Material service dependencies must be visible so that recovery plans address the actual sequence and constraints of service restoration.**

---

# 134. Final Backup Principle

> **Backups are only an effective recovery control when they are protected, sufficiently complete and demonstrably restorable.**

---

# 135. Final Crisis Principle

> **Crisis response must establish clear authority, rapid decision-making, coordinated communications and a controlled transition toward recovery.**

---

# 136. Final Exercise Principle

> **Continuity and recovery plans must be exercised and tested sufficiently to demonstrate practical effectiveness rather than merely documented existence.**

---

# 137. Final Resilience Principle

> **Operational resilience requires the ability to absorb disruption, maintain prioritized services, recover effectively and learn from failures.**

---

# 138. Final Supplier Principle

> **Critical supplier dependencies must be incorporated into continuity, recovery, testing and exit planning.**

---

# 139. Final Assurance Principle

> **Recovery assurance must provide evidence-based confidence that continuity, disaster recovery and crisis arrangements can achieve their intended objectives.**

---

# 140. Final Integration Principle

> **Resilience Governance must integrate with Risk, Service Management, Security, Privacy, Supplier Management, Architecture, Change, Incident Management, Data, Records and Enterprise Assurance.**

---

# 141. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should manage resilience through a controlled lifecycle connecting business impact, critical services, recovery objectives, continuity strategies, dependencies, disaster recovery, crisis management, testing, exercises, supplier continuity and continuous assurance.**
