# 2. Architectural Position

RG-499 established state-transition network intelligence, dynamic arbitration, cross-state resource optimisation, propagation control and autonomous enterprise state orchestration.

RG-500 moves to the **systemic equilibrium layer**. The enterprise is treated as a connected economic, operational and strategic network in which flows of capital, resources, liquidity, capacity, information and value must remain within governed equilibrium ranges.

```text

```

# 3. Core Architectural Question

**How can enterprise continuously maintain systemic equilibrium among capital, liquidity, resources, capacity, information, value, resilience and strategic state while cross-domain flows, shocks, constraints and autonomous actions change the network faster than traditional periodic planning can respond?**

# 4. Governing Principle

**Enterprise network equilibrium SHALL be governed as a system property. No domain SHALL optimise its own flows in a manner that creates unacceptable systemic imbalance elsewhere. Capital, resources, liquidity, capacity and resilience SHALL be synchronised through explicit policies, constraints, evidence and human authority. Autonomous control SHALL remain bounded and reversible wherever feasible.**

```text
STRATEGY
 ↓
SYSTEM CONSTRAINTS
 ↓
NETWORK EQUILIBRIUM
 ↓
FLOW GOVERNANCE
 ↓
RESOURCE / CAPITAL SYNCHRONISATION
 ↓
BOUNDED AUTONOMOUS CONTROL
 ↓
SYSTEM RECONCILIATION
```

# 5. Core Definitions

### Enterprise Network Equilibrium
Governed condition in which aggregate flows, capacities, resources, capital, liquidity, value and resilience remain within approved systemic ranges.

### Systemic Equilibrium
Network-wide balance across multiple interacting enterprise domains.

### Network Disequilibrium
Condition where aggregate demand, exposure, flow or constraint imbalance threatens enterprise objectives or resilience.

### Equilibrium Band
Approved range within which a systemic variable may operate without escalation.

### Equilibrium Threshold
Boundary triggering assessment, rebalancing or escalation.

### Equilibrium Trigger
Evidence-based condition initiating systemic balancing action.

### Cross-Domain Flow
Movement of resources, capital, liquidity, information, capability or value between enterprise domains.

### Flow Object
Governed representation of a measurable enterprise flow.

### Flow Source
Authoritative origin of a flow.

### Flow Destination
Authoritative receiving domain.

### Flow Capacity
Maximum safe or approved flow volume.

### Flow Rate
Rate at which a flow moves through the network.

### Flow Velocity
Speed at which a flow changes state or reaches its destination.

### Flow Friction
Cost, delay or loss associated with movement through the network.

### Flow Elasticity
Ability of a flow to change in response to demand or constraints.

### Flow Congestion
Condition where demand exceeds available flow capacity.

### Flow Bottleneck
Constraint limiting flow throughput.

### Flow Choke Point
Highly central flow dependency with disproportionate systemic impact.

### Flow Interlock
Governed relationship requiring coordinated flow changes.

### Flow Priority
Authorised ordering of competing flows.

### Flow Arbitration
Governed resolution of competing flow demands.

### Flow Substitution
Replacement of one flow path or resource with another acceptable path.

### Flow Rerouting
Controlled movement of a flow through an alternative path.

### Flow Containment
Mechanism limiting propagation of a flow or shock.

### Flow Isolation
Deliberate separation of a flow from part of the network.

### Flow Reconciliation
Comparison of authorised and observed flow.

### Capital Flow
Movement of financial capital among enterprise uses or domains.

### Resource Flow
