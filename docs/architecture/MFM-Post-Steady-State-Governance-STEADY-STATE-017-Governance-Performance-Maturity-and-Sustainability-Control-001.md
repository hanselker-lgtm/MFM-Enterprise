# 1. Purpose

STEADY-STATE-017 establishes the controlled mechanism for assessing the overall performance, maturity and long-term sustainability of the governance system.

The purpose is to determine whether governance is:

```text
OPERATING
EFFECTIVE
CONSISTENT
ADAPTABLE
SUSTAINABLE
```

without confusing these concepts.

The operating sequence is:

```text
GOVERNANCE BASELINE
↓
PERFORMANCE OBSERVATION
↓
EFFECTIVENESS ASSESSMENT
↓
MATURITY ASSESSMENT
↓
SUSTAINABILITY ASSESSMENT
↓
GAP IDENTIFICATION
↓
IMPROVEMENT
↓
REASSESSMENT
```

---

# 2. Core Principle

Performance, effectiveness, maturity and sustainability are related but distinct.

The following distinctions are mandatory:

```text
ACTIVITY
≠
PERFORMANCE

PERFORMANCE
≠
EFFECTIVENESS

EFFECTIVENESS
≠
MATURITY

MATURITY
≠
SUSTAINABILITY

MATURITY
≠
AUTHORIZATION

HIGH MATURITY
≠
GOOD OUTCOME AUTOMATICALLY
```

# 4. Governance Performance

Governance performance concerns how well the governance system operates against defined expectations.

Potential dimensions:

```text
Timeliness
Quality
Consistency
Traceability
Decision Throughput
Control Performance
Exception Performance
Validation Performance
Outcome Performance
Assurance Performance
```

Metrics shall only be formalized where their definitions, sources and interpretation are sufficiently clear.

---

# 5. Performance vs Activity

The existence of activity does not prove performance.

```text
MEETINGS HELD
≠
GOOD GOVERNANCE

DECISIONS MADE
≠
GOOD DECISIONS

CONTROLS EXECUTED
≠
EFFECTIVE CONTROLS
```

Performance shall be assessed against relevant expected outcomes or control objectives.

---

# 6. Governance Effectiveness

Effectiveness concerns whether governance achieves its intended control and decision outcomes.

Possible states:

```text
EFFECTIVE
PARTIALLY EFFECTIVE
INEFFECTIVE
UNVERIFIED
NOT ASSESSED
```

---

# 7. Governance Maturity

Maturity describes the capability and consistency of the governance system, not merely its current results.

A maturity assessment may consider:

```text
Defined
Repeatable
Measured
Integrated
Adaptive
```

These are assessment dimensions, not automatic maturity ratings.

---

# 8. Maturity Assessment Boundary

Maturity shall not be inferred solely from:

```text
Documentation Volume
Number of Controls
Number of Policies
Number of Meetings
Tool Adoption
Organizational Size
```

A large governance framework is not automatically mature.

---

# 9. Sustainability

Governance sustainability concerns whether the governance model can continue to operate effectively without unacceptable dependency, resource strain or structural weakness.

Potential dimensions:

```text
Capacity
Competence
Succession
Funding / Resource Sufficiency
Operational Burden
Technology Support
Knowledge Continuity
Resilience
External Dependency
Improvement Capacity
```

---

# 10. Sustainability Boundary

Sustainability shall distinguish:

```text
CAN OPERATE TODAY
vs
CAN CONTINUE TO OPERATE
```

A governance model that works only through exceptional effort may be effective in the short term but unsustainable.

# 30. Maturity Gap

A maturity gap exists where the governance capability required by its complexity exceeds the observed governance capability.

```text
REQUIRED MATURITY
>
OBSERVED MATURITY
```

It explicitly prevents activity from being mistaken for performance, performance from being mistaken for effectiveness, maturity from being mistaken for effectiveness, and maturity from being mistaken for authorization.
