This document establishes the one-hundred-and-fiftieth document in the MFM v1.2 Steady-State series.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must govern cybersecurity as an enterprise-wide, risk-based, preventive, detective, responsive, resilient and lifecycle-managed capability that protects people, processes, applications, data, infrastructure, networks, cloud services, identities and business services.**

# 3. Cybersecurity Governance Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Establish clear cybersecurity authority, accountability, architecture, standards, risk controls, operational responsibilities and assurance across MFM.**

# 4. Cybersecurity Strategy Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Align cybersecurity investment, controls and operating capabilities with business objectives, enterprise risk, regulatory obligations and threat exposure.**

# 5. Security Architecture Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Embed security into enterprise architecture, applications, data, identity, infrastructure, networks, cloud and integration rather than treating security as an isolated control layer.**

# 6. Security Operations Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Detect, investigate, contain, eradicate and recover from cybersecurity events through disciplined security operations and incident response.**

# 7. Cyber Resilience Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Ensure critical business and technology services can withstand, contain and recover from cybersecurity disruption within defined business requirements.**

# 8. Security Assurance Objective

The primary objective is:

# 100. Final Cybersecurity Governance Principle

> **MFM must govern cybersecurity as an enterprise-wide, risk-based, preventive, detective, responsive, resilient and lifecycle-managed capability that protects people, processes, applications, data, infrastructure, networks, cloud services, identities and business services.**

# 101. Final Security-by-Design Principle

> **Security must be designed into business services, applications, data, identity, infrastructure, networks, cloud and integration from the beginning of their lifecycle.**

# 102. Final Identity Principle

> **Identity must be treated as a foundational security control with strong authentication, least privilege, controlled lifecycle, periodic review and reliable evidence.**

# 103. Final Privileged Access Principle

> **Privileged access must receive enhanced controls, monitoring, review and lifecycle management proportionate to its risk.**

# 104. Final Vulnerability Principle

> **Material vulnerabilities must be identified, risk-prioritized, remediated, validated and supported by evidence, with exceptions formally controlled where remediation cannot be immediate.**

# 105. Final Detection Principle

> **Security monitoring must provide timely, actionable and risk-prioritized detection across identities, endpoints, networks, applications, data, infrastructure and cloud services.**

# 106. Final Incident Response Principle

> **Security incidents must be detected, investigated, contained, eradicated and recovered through controlled processes that preserve appropriate evidence and support continual improvement.**

# 107. Final Cyber Resilience Principle

> **Critical business services must be capable of withstanding, containing and recovering from cybersecurity disruption within defined business requirements.**

# 108. Final Recovery Principle

> **Cyber recovery must protect recovery assets and restore trusted identity, infrastructure, applications and data before business services are declared recovered.**

# 109. Final Security Lifecycle Principle

> **Security capabilities and controls must be actively managed from strategy and design through operation, modernization and secure retirement.**

# 110. Final Assurance Principle

> **Material cybersecurity controls and capabilities must be supported by reliable evidence and periodically assessed through risk-based assurance.**

# 111. Final Improvement Principle
