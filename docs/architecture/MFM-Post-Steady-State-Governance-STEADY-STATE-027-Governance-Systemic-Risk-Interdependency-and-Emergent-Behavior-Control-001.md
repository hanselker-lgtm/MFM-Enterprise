# 1. Purpose

STEADY-STATE-027 establishes the controlled mechanism for identifying, assessing, monitoring and governing systemic risks that arise from interactions, dependencies and feedback across multiple governance domains.

The purpose is to prevent isolated control assessments from creating false confidence when the combined behavior of multiple individually acceptable components creates material systemic exposure.

The operating sequence is:

```text
INDIVIDUAL SIGNALS
↓
CROSS-DOMAIN RELATIONSHIPS
↓
DEPENDENCIES
↓
INTERACTIONS
↓
FEEDBACK LOOPS
↓
EMERGENT BEHAVIOR
↓
SYSTEMIC RISK
↓
SYSTEMIC ASSESSMENT
↓
COORDINATED RESPONSE
↓
REASSESSMENT
↓
LEARNING
```

---

# 2. Core Principle

The following distinctions are mandatory:

```text
LOCAL CONTROL
≠
SYSTEMIC CONTROL

INDIVIDUAL RISK
≠
SYSTEMIC RISK

DEPENDENCY
≠
INTERACTION

CORRELATION
≠
CAUSATION

COMBINED EFFECT
≠
SUM OF INDIVIDUAL EFFECTS

EMERGENT BEHAVIOR
≠
SINGLE-COMPONENT FAILURE

NO LOCAL FAILURE
≠
NO SYSTEMIC RISK

STABLE COMPONENTS
≠
STABLE SYSTEM
```

# 4. Systemic Risk

Systemic risk is risk whose material consequences arise from interactions or dependencies across multiple components, domains or actors.

Systemic risk may exist even when each individual component remains within its local tolerance.

---

# 17. Feedback Loop

A feedback loop exists where system output influences subsequent system behavior.

Examples:

```text
RISK
↓
RESPONSE
↓
CAPACITY CHANGE
↓
NEW RISK
```

or:

```text
DEMAND
↓
CAPACITY PRESSURE
↓
SERVICE DEGRADATION
↓
DEMAND / ESCALATION
```

Material feedback loops should be visible.

---

# 18. Positive Feedback

Positive feedback may amplify change:

```text
SMALL CHANGE
↓
AMPLIFICATION
↓
LARGER CHANGE
↓
FURTHER AMPLIFICATION
```

Positive feedback can create rapid systemic deterioration or growth.

---

# 19. Negative Feedback

Negative feedback may stabilize a system:

```text
DEVIATION
↓
CORRECTIVE RESPONSE
↓
REDUCED DEVIATION
```

The effectiveness of stabilizing feedback should not be assumed.

---

# 20. Emergent Behavior

Emergent behavior is system behavior that cannot be adequately explained by examining one component in isolation.

Possible examples:

```text
Unexpected Bottleneck
Unexpected Risk Concentration
Unexpected Capacity Collapse
Unexpected Demand Shift
Unexpected Control Interaction
Unexpected Cost Escalation
```

Emergent behavior should be treated as an analytical signal, not automatically as proof of a specific cause.

---

# 21. Non-Linearity

System responses may be non-linear.

Therefore:

```text
SMALL INPUT CHANGE
≠
SMALL OUTPUT CHANGE
```

Where material, analysis should consider thresholds, tipping points and amplification.

---

# 23. Tipping Point

A tipping point is a threshold beyond which recovery or stabilization may become substantially more difficult.

Tipping-point analysis should remain evidence-based and should clearly distinguish observation from scenario assumptions.
