# 2. Purpose

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-429 defines the action and outcome layer above recurrence and systemic-risk detection.

RG-428 establishes how recurring patterns and systemic risks are identified.

RG-429 establishes **how a systemic condition is converted into an accountable intervention and how the organisation proves whether the intervention actually changed the underlying risk**.

The architecture SHALL distinguish:

```text
SYSTEMIC REMEDIATION
= COORDINATED ACTION ADDRESSING A CONDITION ACROSS A DEFINED POPULATION

ENTERPRISE INTERVENTION
= GOVERNED ACTION WITH CROSS-DOMAIN OR ENTERPRISE EFFECT

OUTCOME
= OBSERVABLE RESULT OF AN INTERVENTION

OUTCOME TARGET
= DEFINED CONDITION THE INTERVENTION IS INTENDED TO ACHIEVE

EFFECTIVENESS
= EVIDENCE THAT THE INTERVENTION PRODUCED THE INTENDED RISK REDUCTION OR CONTROL IMPROVEMENT

BENEFIT
= POSITIVE VALUE GENERATED BY THE INTERVENTION

RESIDUAL RISK
= RISK REMAINING AFTER INTERVENTION

OUTCOME VERIFICATION
= CONTROLLED CONFIRMATION THAT THE CLAIMED RESULT IS SUPPORTED BY EVIDENCE
```

---

# 3. Core Principle

> **A systemic intervention is not successful because actions were completed; it is successful when evidence demonstrates that the targeted systemic condition, exposure or risk has materially changed.**

The governing chain is:

```text
SYSTEMIC RISK
      ↓
INTERVENTION OBJECTIVE
      ↓
TARGET OUTCOME
      ↓
INTERVENTION DESIGN
      ↓
AUTHORITY
      ↓
IMPLEMENTATION
      ↓
POPULATION CHANGE
      ↓
MEASUREMENT
      ↓
OUTCOME VERIFICATION
      ↓
EFFECTIVENESS
      ↓
CLOSE / CONTINUE / ADAPT
```

# 133. Acceptance Criteria

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-429 is accepted when:

- systemic remediation is distinguished from individual remediation;
- enterprise interventions have explicit objectives and populations;
- baseline, target and measurement are governed;
- outcome and benefit are distinct;
- leading and lagging indicators are supported;
- counterfactual and attribution considerations are supported;
- intervention dependencies and sequencing are controlled;
- material interventions have authority and accountable ownership;
- pilot and phased rollout are supported;
- outcome verification is independent where appropriate;
- effectiveness is distinct from completion;
- unintended consequences and side effects are tracked;
- metric and baseline manipulation are controlled;
- residual risk remains visible;
- systemic risk is not closed merely because intervention actions are complete;
- AI-assisted outcome analysis is governed;
- portfolio-level change saturation and intervention collision are visible;
- historical outcome integrity is preserved;
- negative tests prevent unsupported success claims and premature systemic closure.

# 135. Governing Principle

> **An intervention is only effective when the measured population-level outcome demonstrates meaningful change, the evidence supports the attribution, unintended consequences are understood, and the improvement remains sustainable after implementation.**

The PC-RG architecture SHALL therefore distinguish action completion from outcome achievement, outcome achievement from effectiveness, and effectiveness from durable systemic improvement.
