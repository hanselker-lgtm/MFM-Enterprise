# 1. Purpose

STEADY-STATE-023 establishes the controlled mechanism for evaluating decision alternatives, trade-offs, constraints, consequences, reversibility and decision quality before an authorized governance decision is made.

The purpose is to ensure that decisions are not reduced to simple preference or model output, but are evaluated against evidence, objectives, risk, value, constraints and consequences.

The operating sequence is:

```text
DECISION QUESTION
↓
OPTIONS
↓
CRITERIA
↓
EVIDENCE
↓
CONSTRAINTS
↓
TRADE-OFF ANALYSIS
↓
CONSEQUENCE ANALYSIS
↓
RISK / VALUE ASSESSMENT
↓
RECOMMENDATION
↓
AUTHORIZATION
↓
DECISION QUALITY REVIEW
↓
OUTCOME / LEARNING
```

---

# 2. Core Principle

Decision quality is not the same as decision outcome.

The following distinctions are mandatory:

```text
GOOD DECISION
≠
GOOD OUTCOME

BAD OUTCOME
≠
BAD DECISION

RECOMMENDATION
≠
DECISION

PREFERENCE
≠
EVIDENCE

OPTION
≠
COMMITMENT

TRADE-OFF
≠
ERROR

MODEL RESULT
≠
DECISION AUTHORITY

CONSENSUS
≠
CORRECTNESS
```

# 6. Decision Options

Material decisions should identify reasonable alternatives.

Possible option classes:

```text
OPTION A
OPTION B
```

# 11. Criteria Weighting

Where weighted evaluation is used:

```text
WEIGHT
+
SCORE
+
EVIDENCE
=
```

# 12. Scoring

Where scoring is used, the scale shall be defined.

Example:

```text

2 = Poor
3 = Acceptable
4 = Good
5 = Very Good
```

The actual scale may vary by decision.

# 13. Scoring Boundary

Scores are decision-support instruments.

```text
HIGH SCORE
≠
AUTOMATIC APPROVAL

LOW SCORE
≠
AUTOMATIC REJECTION
```

Material judgment shall remain visible.

# 15. Trade-Off

A trade-off exists where improving one decision objective creates a cost, reduction or constraint in another.

Examples:

```text
Cost vs Speed
Risk vs Value
Flexibility vs Control
Resilience vs Efficiency
Standardization vs Local Adaptation
Short-Term vs Long-Term Value
```

Trade-offs shall be made explicit where material.

---

# 16. Trade-Off Transparency

A decision paper should not hide material trade-offs behind a single aggregate score.

Where material, it should identify:

```text
Benefit
Cost
Risk
Opportunity
Dependency
Uncertainty
Residual Effect
```

---

# 17. Hard Constraints

Some decision criteria are constraints rather than preferences.

Examples:

```text
Mandatory Requirement
Authority Limit
Safety Requirement
Budget Ceiling
Capacity Limit
Technical Boundary
Continuity Requirement
```

A hard constraint shall not be treated as an ordinary weighted preference.

---

# 18. Constraint Failure

Where an option violates a material hard constraint:

```text
OPTION
=
NON-COMPLIANT
```

unless the applicable authority explicitly changes or waives the constraint through a controlled mechanism.

# 44. Decision Quality vs Outcome

A decision can be high quality even when the outcome is unfavorable due to uncertainty or external events.

Conversely, a favorable outcome can result from a poor decision combined with favorable circumstances.

Therefore:

```text
DECISION QUALITY
AND
OUTCOME QUALITY
```

shall be assessed separately.
