# 1. Purpose

This document defines the seventy-sixth implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-75 – Data Governance, Data Quality, Master Data, Information Lifecycle, Metadata, Data Lineage & Data Assurance Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish a controlled privacy governance and privacy operations capability covering personal data governance, lawful purpose, privacy classification, consent, data subject rights, data subject request management, privacy impact assessment, privacy by design, data minimization, retention, sharing, controller / processor governance, privacy incidents, privacy monitoring and evidence-based privacy assurance.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must ensure that personal data is processed lawfully, fairly, transparently, securely and only for defined purposes, while enabling applicable data subject rights and maintaining evidence-based privacy governance throughout the information lifecycle.**

# 115. Definition of Ready

A privacy governance work item is Ready when:

- Processing scope is defined.
- Purpose is known.
- Data categories and data subjects are identified.
- Owner is assigned.
- Applicable privacy requirements are identified.
- Lawful basis is assessed.
- Retention and sharing considerations are known.
- Required DPIA or privacy assessment is identified.

---

# 117. Final Privacy Governance Principle

> **Personal data processing must be governed from purpose and lawful basis through use, sharing, retention, rights and secure disposal.**

---

# 118. Final Transparency Principle

> **Individuals should receive clear and appropriate information about how their personal data is processed and how applicable rights can be exercised.**

---

# 119. Final Consent Principle

> **Where consent is relied upon, the organization must be able to demonstrate what was consented to, when, under which information and how withdrawal is handled.**

---

# 120. Final Rights Principle

> **Data subject rights must be managed through a controlled, traceable and deadline-aware operational process.**

---

# 121. Final Privacy by Design Principle

> **Privacy must be considered during architecture, process, data, application, integration, change and release design rather than added only after implementation.**

---

# 122. Final Minimization Principle

> **Personal data should be limited to what is reasonably necessary for the defined purpose.**

---

# 123. Final Retention Principle

> **Personal data should not be retained longer than justified by purpose, legal requirement or legitimate operational need.**

---

# 124. Final Sharing Principle

> **Personal data sharing must be purposeful, authorized, protected and appropriately governed throughout the recipient relationship.**

---

# 125. Final Breach Principle

> **Potential personal data breaches must be assessed rapidly and proportionately, with escalation and notification handled according to applicable requirements.**

---

# 126. Final Assurance Principle

> **Privacy assurance must provide evidence-based confidence that personal data processing, rights, consent, retention, sharing and incident controls operate as intended.**

---

# 127. Final Integration Principle

> **Privacy Governance must integrate with Data Governance, Security, Records, Identity, Service, Incident, Change, Supplier, Risk, Compliance and Enterprise Assurance governance.**

---

# 128. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should manage privacy through a controlled lifecycle connecting processing purpose, lawful basis, transparency, consent, data subject rights, privacy assessment, minimization, retention, sharing, incident response and continuous assurance.**

**Procurement Governance Authority:** Procurement / Supplier / Contract / Vendor Lifecycle / Third-Party Risk / Supply-Chain Assurance  
**Enterprise Assurance Authority:** Risk / Compliance / Internal Control / Audit / Policy / Enterprise Assurance  
**Configuration Governance Authority:** Configuration Management / Asset Management / CMDB / Dependency Mapping / Technology Lifecycle Assurance  
**Principle:** MFM must ensure that personal data is processed lawfully, fairly, transparently, securely and only for defined purposes, while enabling applicable data subject rights and maintaining evidence-based privacy governance throughout the information lifecycle
