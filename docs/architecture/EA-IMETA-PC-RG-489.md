# 2. Architectural Position

RG-488 established multi-horizon foresight, scenario navigation, adaptive option portfolios and anticipatory decision orchestration.

RG-489 addresses the next architectural problem: **when several possible futures begin to converge sufficiently to justify commitment, how should the enterprise price the remaining options, manage the decision window and transition from preparation to controlled commitment?**

```text
RG-487 REGIME INTELLIGENCE
        ↓
RG-488 FORESIGHT + OPTION PORTFOLIOS
        ↓
RG-489 FUTURE-STATE CONVERGENCE
        ↓
OPTION ECONOMICS
        ↓
DECISION-WINDOW ORCHESTRATION
        ↓
ADAPTIVE COMMITMENT
        ↓
NEW EQUILIBRIUM
```

# 3. Core Architectural Question

**How can the enterprise recognise when uncertainty has narrowed enough to justify commitment, quantify the economic value of remaining options, coordinate decision windows across capital, resources, capacity, liquidity and strategy, and commit without creating uncontrolled lock-in?**

RG-489 therefore treats convergence, option economics, commitment thresholds, timing windows, dependency readiness and adaptive commitment as first-class architectural concerns.

# 4. Governing Principle

**The enterprise SHALL preserve optionality while uncertainty is material, SHALL progressively increase commitment as evidence and readiness converge, and SHALL only cross material commitment thresholds when economic value, timing, resilience, strategy and authority are jointly satisfied.**

```text
UNCERTAINTY
    ↓
SCENARIO CONVERGENCE
    ↓
OPTION VALUE
    ↓
DECISION WINDOW
    ↓
READINESS
    ↓
COMMITMENT THRESHOLD
    ↓
AUTHORISED COMMITMENT
    ↓
ADAPTIVE EXECUTION
    ↓
RECONCILIATION
```

# 7. Future-State Convergence Model

RG-489 introduces a controlled transition:

```text
SCENARIO A ─────┐
SCENARIO B ─────┼──→ CONVERGENCE ZONE
SCENARIO C ─────┤
SCENARIO D ─────┘
                     ↓
             DECISION SUFFICIENCY
                     ↓
             COMMITMENT WINDOW
```

Convergence SHALL be evaluated by decision relevance rather than requiring identical forecasts. Several futures may remain different while producing the same acceptable decision.

# 8. Convergence Classes

Convergence SHOULD be classified as:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Structural | Underlying economic relationships converge |
| Outcome | Material outcomes become similar |
| Decision | Same decision is preferred across scenarios |
| Timing | Decision window becomes common |
| Resource | Required resource/capacity conditions converge |
| Strategic | Strategic implications converge |
| Temporary | Short-lived apparent convergence |

Temporary convergence SHALL not automatically justify irreversible commitment.

# 9. Convergence Confidence

Confidence SHALL consider:

```text
SIGNAL INDEPENDENCE
MODEL AGREEMENT
SCENARIO PERSISTENCE
DATA QUALITY
REGIME CONFIDENCE
TIME HORIZON
EXTERNAL DEPENDENCIES
```

High convergence confidence does not override strategy or authority.

# 10. Decision Sufficiency

Decision sufficiency exists when additional information is unlikely to materially alter:

- decision choice;
- timing;
- commitment size;
- risk posture;
- option portfolio;
- authority requirement.

The architecture SHALL distinguish **sufficient evidence** from **complete certainty**. Complete certainty is not required for most decisions.

# 11. Residual Uncertainty

Residual uncertainty SHALL remain explicit after convergence.

```text
TOTAL UNCERTAINTY
        ↓
REDUCED BY EVIDENCE
        ↓
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY
        ↓
COMMITMENT ROBUSTNESS
```

Residual uncertainty SHALL determine the size, staging and reversibility of commitment.

# 12. Option Economics

Option economics extends RG-488 by pricing the value of remaining choices.

A simplified decision representation is:

```text
NET COMMITMENT VALUE
=
EXPECTED COMMITMENT VALUE
− COMMITMENT COST
− COMMITMENT RISK
− OPTIONALITY LOST
+ TIMING BENEFIT
+ STRATEGIC CAPTURE VALUE
```

The architecture SHALL avoid treating these terms as universally additive; domain-specific valuation models remain governed.

# 13. Option Carry Cost

Maintaining an option may consume:

- capital;
- liquidity;
- management attention;
- capacity;
- supplier commitments;
- technology resources;
- organisational readiness.

Carry cost SHALL be compared with the option's expected future value.

# 14. Option Exercise Value

Exercise value SHALL consider:

- expected economic value;
- strategic value;
- avoided disruption;
- capacity security;
- resource security;
- timing advantage;
- competitive position;
- residual flexibility.

# 15. Option Interaction

Options SHALL be evaluated as portfolios rather than isolated choices.

```text
OPTION A + OPTION B → SYNERGY
OPTION A + OPTION C → CONFLICT
OPTION B → ENABLES OPTION D
OPTION C → BLOCKS OPTION E
```

Material interactions SHALL be visible before activation.

# 16. Commitment Economics

Commitment creates value by converting optionality into capability, capacity or strategic position.

Commitment also creates exposure.

Therefore:

```text
OPTIONALITY VALUE
        ↓
COMMITMENT DECISION
        ↓
CAPTURED VALUE
        +
LOCK-IN EXPOSURE
```

A commitment SHALL be evaluated for both captured value and lost flexibility.

# 17. Staged Commitment

Where uncertainty remains material, commitments SHOULD be staged.

```text
TRANCHE 0 → PREPARATION
TRANCHE 1 → LIMITED COMMITMENT
TRANCHE 2 → SCALE
TRANCHE 3 → FULL COMMITMENT
```

Each tranche SHALL have independent release criteria where material.

# 18. Commitment Corridor

The commitment corridor defines the acceptable range:

```text
COMMITMENT FLOOR
       │
       ├── SAFE ADAPTIVE RANGE
       │
       ├── TARGET
       │
       ├── CONDITIONAL RANGE
       │
       └── COMMITMENT CEILING
```

Autonomous systems SHALL not exceed the corridor.

A commitment remains strategically valid only while it remains aligned with authorised objectives.

Economic attractiveness SHALL NOT override a strategic contradiction.

- available;
- scarce;
- shared;
- strategically reserved;
- substitutable;
- exposed to external disruption.

Commitment shall not create hidden capacity starvation elsewhere in the enterprise.

# 29. Commitment Cascade

A commitment cascade occurs when one commitment creates mandatory downstream commitments.

```text
COMMIT A
 ↓
CAPACITY REQUIREMENT
 ↓
RESOURCE COMMITMENT
 ↓
CAPITAL COMMITMENT
 ↓
SUPPORTING TECHNOLOGY
 ↓
OPERATING COMMITMENT
```

Cascade depth and exposure SHALL be visible before material commitment.

# 30. Commitment Cannibalisation and Synergy

The architecture SHALL identify where commitments compete for the same:

- capital;
- liquidity;
- capacity;
- scarce resources;
- management attention;
- strategic bandwidth.

It SHALL also identify synergistic commitments that increase combined value.

# 31. Adaptive Commitment

Adaptive commitment SHALL define which parameters may change without reopening the strategic decision.

```text
STRATEGIC INTENT = FIXED
EXECUTION PARAMETERS = ADAPTIVE
```

Examples of adaptive parameters MAY include sequencing, tranche size, timing, supplier mix and operational configuration, subject to governance.

# 32. Commitment Reversal

Reversal SHALL be planned where feasible.

A reversal plan SHALL define:

- trigger;
- maximum exposure;
- authority;
- cost;
- residual obligations;
- affected dependencies;
- recovery action.

Irreversible commitments SHALL receive elevated scrutiny.

# 33. Commitment Circuit Breakers

Circuit breakers SHALL stop escalation when:

- evidence deteriorates;
- convergence reverses;
- liquidity falls below floor;
- resilience margin is threatened;
- security conditions change;
- authority becomes invalid;
- dependency failure occurs;
- commitment exposure exceeds corridor.

# 34. Safe State

When commitment intelligence or authority becomes unreliable:

```text
STOP ESCALATION
 ↓
PROTECT LIQUIDITY
 ↓
PROTECT RESILIENCE
 ↓
PRESERVE OPTIONS
 ↓
RECONCILE STATE
 ↓
RESTORE AUTHORITY
```

The safe state SHALL preserve the ability to resume without forcing unnecessary commitment.

# 35. AI and Autonomous Commitment

AI MAY:

- identify convergence;
- calculate option economics;
- monitor decision clocks;
- prepare evidence packs;
- recommend tranche releases;
- detect dependency readiness;
- simulate commitment alternatives;
- propose reversal triggers.

AI SHALL NOT:

- change strategy;
- redefine commitment ceilings;
- lower liquidity or resilience floors;
- create authority;
- activate material strategic commitment outside its envelope;
- suppress residual uncertainty.

# 36. Autonomous Commitment Envelope

The autonomous envelope SHALL specify:

```text
MAX COMMITMENT
MAX INCREASE RATE
MAX LIQUIDITY EXPOSURE
MAX CAPITAL EXPOSURE
MAX RESOURCE EXPOSURE
MAX STRATEGIC SENSITIVITY
MINIMUM CONVERGENCE CONFIDENCE
MINIMUM READINESS
MAXIMUM IRREVERSIBILITY
REQUIRED HUMAN GATE
```

Any boundary breach SHALL block or escalate the action.
