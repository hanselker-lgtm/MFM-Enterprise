# 2. Architectural Position

RG-494 established outcome-strategy alignment, benefit portfolio convergence, cross-domain value synchronisation and strategic benefit resilience.

RG-495 extends this architecture into **multi-horizon strategic value governance**. The enterprise must avoid optimising the present at the expense of the future while also avoiding excessive investment in distant possibilities that undermines current resilience and liquidity.

```text
RG-493 VALUE-OUTCOME NETWORKS
        ↓
RG-494 OUTCOME-STRATEGY ALIGNMENT
        ↓
RG-495 STRATEGIC VALUE SYSTEMS
        ↓
MULTI-HORIZON PORTFOLIO GOVERNANCE
        ↓
OPTION PRESERVATION
        ↓
RESILIENCE BALANCING
        ↓
AUTONOMIC STRATEGIC VALUE ORCHESTRATION
```

# 3. Core Architectural Question

**How can the enterprise continuously balance near-term value, medium-term strategic transformation and long-term strategic optionality while preserving resilience, liquidity, capacity and human authority, and while preventing autonomous optimisation from collapsing the enterprise into short-term local objectives?**

# 4. Governing Principle

**Enterprise value SHALL be governed across multiple strategic horizons. Near-term performance SHALL not consume the capacity required for future strategic viability, while long-term optionality SHALL not be allowed to undermine current resilience or hard constraints. Autonomous orchestration SHALL optimise within approved horizon-specific envelopes and SHALL never redefine strategy.**

```text
CURRENT PERFORMANCE
        +
TRANSFORMATION
        +
FUTURE OPTION VALUE
        ↓
MULTI-HORIZON STRATEGIC VALUE
        ↓
RESILIENCE + LIQUIDITY + OPTIONALITY
        ↓
ENTERPRISE BALANCE
```

# 7. Multi-Horizon Architecture

The strategic value system SHALL distinguish at least three strategic horizons.

```text
HORIZON 0
CURRENT PERFORMANCE
        ↓
HORIZON 1
NEAR-TERM TRANSFORMATION
        ↓
HORIZON 2
MEDIUM-TERM CAPABILITY
        ↓
HORIZON 3
LONG-TERM OPTIONS
```

The horizons are complementary rather than mutually exclusive.

# 8. Horizon 0 — Current Value

Horizon 0 protects:

- continuity;
- current commitments;
- liquidity;
- operational performance;
- customer and stakeholder obligations;
- critical resilience.

Horizon 0 SHALL not automatically consume all discretionary capacity.

# 9. Horizon 1 — Near-Term Transformation

Horizon 1 focuses on:

- scaling;
- process transformation;
- measurable capability improvements;
- near-term strategic outcomes;
- conversion of approved opportunities into value.

# 10. Horizon 2 — Medium-Term Capability

Horizon 2 focuses on:

- new capabilities;
- operating-model evolution;
- market development;
- technology transition;
- strategic infrastructure;
- emerging business models.

# 11. Horizon 3 — Long-Term Options

Horizon 3 protects:

- future strategic options;
- discontinuity readiness;
- exploratory capability;
- strategic experiments;
- future market positions.

Horizon 3 SHALL remain bounded by governance and affordability.

# 12. Horizon Balance

The enterprise SHALL balance:

```text
CURRENT VALUE
+
TRANSFORMATION VALUE
+
FUTURE OPTION VALUE
+
RESILIENCE
```

No single horizon SHALL be treated as universally superior.

# 13. Horizon Conflict

Horizon conflict SHALL be explicit when current returns compete with future capability or options.

Governance SHALL expose:

- value;
- opportunity cost;
- resilience;
- liquidity;
- reversibility.

# 14. Horizon Convergence

Convergence occurs when investments across horizons reinforce one strategic trajectory.

Example:

```text
H0 OPERATE
 ↓
H1 TRANSFORM
 ↓
H2 BUILD CAPABILITY
 ↓
H3 PRESERVE OPTION
```

The architecture SHOULD identify reinforcing chains.

# 15. Horizon Cannibalisation

A horizon may consume scarce resources required elsewhere.

Cannibalisation SHALL be measured rather than assumed.

# 16. Strategic Option Architecture

Strategic options SHALL include:

- trigger;
- expiry;
- preservation cost;
- exercise cost;
- readiness;
- authority;
- expected value;
- downside;
- dependency.

# 17. Option Preservation

Option preservation SHALL balance:

```text
OPTION VALUE
−
OPTION COST
−
OPTION RISK
```

Options SHALL not be preserved indefinitely without reassessment.

# 18. Option Exercise

Exercise SHALL require:

- trigger satisfaction;
- strategic fit;
- capacity;
- capital;
- resilience;
- authority.

Autonomous systems MAY recommend exercise but SHALL not exercise strategic options beyond their authority envelope.

# 19. Option Expiry

Expired options SHALL be removed or explicitly renewed.

Historical option state SHALL remain auditable.

# 20. Option Readiness

Readiness SHALL consider:

- capability maturity;
- capital availability;
- resource availability;
- decision readiness;
- supplier readiness;
- implementation lead time.

# 21. Irreversibility

High-irreversibility decisions SHALL require stronger governance.

```text
LOW REVERSIBILITY
        ↓
HIGH GOVERNANCE REQUIREMENT
```

Autonomous authority SHALL narrow as irreversibility increases.

# 22. Strategic Flexibility

Flexibility is the ability to change course without violating hard constraints.

Portfolio design SHOULD preserve multiple viable paths where uncertainty is material.

# 23. Strategic Robustness

Robust strategies perform acceptably across multiple scenarios.

Robustness SHALL be evaluated alongside expected value.

# 24. Strategic Resilience

Resilience SHALL protect:

- critical outcomes;
- liquidity;
- operational continuity;
- strategic capacity;
- future options.

# 25. Resilience Budget

A resilience budget SHALL identify capacity intentionally reserved for disruption.

The budget SHALL not be silently reallocated to discretionary value generation.

# 26. Resilience Premium

The enterprise MAY accept lower short-term economic efficiency to preserve critical resilience.

Such trade-offs SHALL be explicit.

# 27. Liquidity and Option Trade-off

Liquidity provides resilience and optionality.

```text
LIQUIDITY
 ↔
OPTION PRESERVATION
 ↔
STRATEGIC INVESTMENT
```

No strategic option SHALL justify violating approved liquidity floors.

# 28. Capital and Horizon Trade-off

Capital allocation SHALL consider:

- immediate return;
- transformation value;
- option value;
- risk;
- duration;
- irreversibility.

# 29. Capacity and Horizon Trade-off

Capacity allocation SHALL prevent current delivery demand from eliminating future capability-building capacity.

# 30. Strategic Value Density

Strategic value density MAY inform scarce-capacity allocation but SHALL not override strategic priorities, resilience or hard constraints.

# 31. Strategic Value Velocity

Value velocity SHALL measure how quickly strategic value becomes realised.

High velocity SHALL not automatically outrank durable long-term value.

# 32. Strategic Value Duration

Strategic value duration SHALL measure expected persistence.

Short-lived value SHALL be distinguished from durable value.

# 33. Strategic Value Decay

Value decay SHALL be monitored across horizons.

Long-horizon opportunities may decay if the enterprise delays action beyond a strategic window.

# 34. Strategic Value Concentration

Concentration SHALL be measured by:

- horizon;
- domain;
- capability;
- technology;
- supplier;
- strategic assumption;
- option.

# 35. Strategic Value Diversification

# 48. Autonomous Strategic Value Orchestration

Autonomous systems MAY:

- monitor horizon balance;
- identify strategic drift;
- forecast option decay;
- recommend reallocation;
- coordinate approved horizon transitions;
- simulate scenario impacts;
- detect concentration;
- recommend resilience actions.

They SHALL NOT:

- redefine strategy;
- change strategic priorities;
- lower hard constraints;
- release protected reserves without authority;
- eliminate strategic options solely for short-term gain;
- create new decision rights.
