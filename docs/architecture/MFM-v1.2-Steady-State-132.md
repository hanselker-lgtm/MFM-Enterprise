# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the one-hundred-and-thirty-second document in the MFM v1.2 Steady-State series.

It follows MFM v1.2-Steady-State-131 – Enterprise Service Management Architecture, ITSM Governance, Service Portfolio, Service Catalogue, Incident Management, Problem Management, Request Management, Change Management, Release Management, Configuration Management, Asset Management, Knowledge Management, Service Continuity & ITSM Assurance.

The purpose is to establish the permanent enterprise operating model for cybersecurity strategy, security governance, security architecture, security operations, security monitoring, threat intelligence, threat detection, vulnerability management, security engineering, security incident response, security investigations, security testing, security configuration, endpoint security, network security, cloud security, application security, data security, identity security, security suppliers, security compliance, security exceptions, remediation, cyber resilience, security assurance, metrics, dashboards, maturity and continual enterprise cybersecurity capability improvement.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must govern cybersecurity as an integrated enterprise capability that protects business services, identities, applications, data, infrastructure, networks, cloud services and suppliers through risk-based prevention, detection, response, recovery, assurance and continual improvement.**

# 3. Cybersecurity Governance Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Establish clear authority, accountability, policy, architecture, risk ownership, security controls, monitoring, response and assurance for enterprise cybersecurity.**

# 4. Cybersecurity Architecture Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Provide coherent security architecture across business services, applications, data, identity, infrastructure, network, cloud, endpoints, suppliers and operational environments.**

# 5. Security Operations Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Continuously monitor, detect, analyze, contain and respond to cybersecurity threats and security events according to risk and business impact.**

# 6. Vulnerability Management Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Identify, prioritize, remediate and verify security vulnerabilities according to exploitability, exposure, business criticality and risk.**

# 7. Security Incident Response Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Contain and recover from cybersecurity incidents while preserving evidence, minimizing business impact and preventing recurrence.**

# 8. Cyber Resilience Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Ensure critical business services can withstand, respond to and recover from cyber disruption within defined business requirements.**

# 9. Security Assurance Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Provide reliable evidence that cybersecurity controls, processes and services operate effectively and remain aligned with enterprise risk and requirements.**

# 120. Definition of Ready

A security architecture, control, detection, vulnerability remediation, security incident, security test, security engineering change, cyber recovery arrangement, supplier decision, exception, remediation or assurance review is Ready when purpose, owner, scope, affected assets or services, threat/risk context, dependencies, security requirements, recovery requirements, approval authority and acceptance criteria are defined.

# 122. Final Cybersecurity Governance Principle

> **MFM must govern cybersecurity as an integrated enterprise capability that protects business services, identities, applications, data, infrastructure, networks, cloud services and suppliers through risk-based prevention, detection, response, recovery, assurance and continual improvement.**

# 123. Final Security Architecture Principle

> **Security must be engineered into business services, applications, data, identity, infrastructure, networks, cloud and supplier relationships rather than treated solely as an operational afterthought.**

# 124. Final Detection Principle

> **Material threats must be detectable through appropriate telemetry, analytics, detection logic, monitoring and operational response capabilities.**

# 125. Final Vulnerability Principle

> **Vulnerabilities must be prioritized according to exploitability, exposure, asset criticality and business impact rather than severity alone.**

# 126. Final Incident Principle

> **Cybersecurity incidents must be contained, investigated, eradicated and recovered through coordinated response while preserving appropriate evidence and minimizing business impact.**

# 127. Final Resilience Principle

> **Critical business services must have cyber-resilience and recovery capabilities that remain viable even when identity, infrastructure, applications or data have been compromised.**

# 128. Final Security Assurance Principle

> **Material cybersecurity controls and services must be supported by reliable evidence and periodically assessed through risk-based assurance.**

# 129. Final Improvement Principle

> **Security incidents, vulnerabilities, detection gaps, test findings, configuration weaknesses, recovery results and assurance findings must continuously improve MFM's cybersecurity capability.**

# 130. Final Enterprise Cybersecurity Integration Principle

> **Cybersecurity must integrate with Enterprise Architecture, Business Architecture, Applications, Data, Identity, Service Management, Infrastructure, Network, Cloud, Configuration Management, Asset Management, Suppliers, Risk, Compliance, Legal, Privacy and Business Continuity.**

# 131. Final Steady-State Cybersecurity Principle

> **MFM must govern cybersecurity as an integrated enterprise capability that protects business services, identities, applications, data, infrastructure, networks, cloud services and suppliers through risk-based prevention, detection, response, recovery, assurance and continual improvement.**
