# 2. Purpose

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-467 establishes the strategic investment layer above the capability-rebaselining and future-readiness architecture defined by RG-466.

RG-466 establishes how validated learning becomes capability, how capability is rebaselined, how regression is prevented and how future readiness is stress-tested.

RG-467 establishes how the enterprise decides where to invest scarce resilience capital, management attention, technology capacity, people, reserves and strategic effort across competing present and future needs.

The architecture SHALL answer:

> **How does the enterprise allocate scarce resilience investment across uncertain futures so that current vulnerabilities are reduced, future capabilities are developed, strategic options are preserved and investment remains proportionate to exposure, value, uncertainty and capacity?**

The architecture SHALL distinguish:

```text
RESILIENCE INVESTMENT
= RESOURCE COMMITMENT INTENDED TO INCREASE THE ENTERPRISE'S ABILITY TO ABSORB, ADAPT, RESPOND, RECOVER OR LEARN

INVESTMENT POOL
= GOVERNED SET OF RESOURCES AVAILABLE FOR RESILIENCE INVESTMENT

INVESTMENT CASE
= STRUCTURED JUSTIFICATION FOR A RESILIENCE INVESTMENT

INVESTMENT OPTION
= ALTERNATIVE WAY TO ADDRESS A CAPABILITY, RISK OR FUTURE-READINESS NEED

INVESTMENT VALUE
= EXPECTED NET RESILIENCE, STRATEGIC OR BUSINESS VALUE CREATED BY AN INVESTMENT

RESILIENCE RETURN
= EXPECTED RESILIENCE BENEFIT RELATIVE TO INVESTMENT

INVESTMENT HORIZON
= PERIOD OVER WHICH AN INVESTMENT IS EXPECTED TO CREATE VALUE OR REQUIRE COMMITMENT

INVESTMENT TIMING
= DECISION ABOUT WHEN TO COMMIT RESOURCES

INVESTMENT STAGING
= DELIBERATE PHASING OF AN INVESTMENT TO PRESERVE INFORMATION AND OPTIONALITY

INVESTMENT GATE

INVESTMENT TRANCHE
= CONTROLLED PORTION OF AN INVESTMENT RELEASED AGAINST CONDITIONS

= RESOURCE OBLIGATION CREATED BY AN APPROVED INVESTMENT

INVESTMENT EXPOSURE
= RISK CREATED BY COMMITTING RESOURCES TO AN INVESTMENT

INVESTMENT REVERSIBILITY
= DEGREE TO WHICH AN INVESTMENT CAN BE STOPPED OR REDIRECTED WITHOUT MATERIAL LOSS

SUNSET CONDITION
= DEFINED CONDITION UNDER WHICH AN INVESTMENT OR CAPABILITY SHOULD END

= VALUE CREATED BY PRESERVING VIABLE FUTURE CHOICES

= EXPECTED VALUE OF RETAINING THE ABILITY TO CHOOSE LATER

= INVESTMENT THAT CREATES A FUTURE CHOICE WITHOUT REQUIRING FULL COMMITMENT TODAY

= VALUE OF DELAYING COMMITMENT TO GAIN INFORMATION OR AVOID LOCK-IN

= VALUE CREATED BY AN INVESTMENT THAT REDUCES UNCERTAINTY

= LIMITED INVESTMENT USED TO TEST AN APPROACH BEFORE SCALING

= INITIAL INVESTMENT INTENDED TO CREATE A FUTURE CAPABILITY OR OPTION

= INVESTMENT USED TO EXTEND A VERIFIED CAPABILITY

= GOVERNED COLLECTION OF INVESTMENTS MANAGED TO ACHIEVE A COMBINED OBJECTIVE

= DISTRIBUTION OF INVESTMENT ACROSS TIME HORIZONS, risks, capabilities and scenarios

= DEGREE TO WHICH MULTIPLE INVESTMENTS FAIL OR SUCCEED UNDER THE SAME CONDITIONS

= RISK CREATED BY RELIANCE ON A PARTICULAR capability, supplier, technology, market or assumption

= RISK ARISING FROM EXCESSIVE DEPENDENCE ON A LIMITED NUMBER OF FACTORS

INVESTMENT BOTTLENECK
= CONSTRAINT LIMITING THE ENTERPRISE'S ABILITY TO EXECUTE OR SCALE RESILIENCE INVESTMENT

CAPACITY ENVELOPE
= PRACTICAL LIMIT OF INVESTMENT, implementation and change capacity

= RESOURCE COMMITMENT REQUIRED TO IMPLEMENT AN INVESTMENT

= VALUE LOST BECAUSE A DIFFERENT INVESTMENT TIMING OR OPTION WOULD HAVE PRODUCED A better outcome

UNDERINVESTMENT
= CONDITION WHERE REQUIRED resilience capability IS NOT ADEQUATELY FUNDED OR DEVELOPED

OVERINVESTMENT
= CONDITION WHERE RESOURCE COMMITMENT EXCEEDS JUSTIFIED NEED OR VALUE

INVESTMENT DEBT
= REQUIRED INVESTMENT DEFERRED BEYOND AN ACCEPTABLE POINT

READINESS RESERVE
= RESOURCE OR CAPABILITY DELIBERATELY MAINTAINED FOR FUTURE RESPONSE

= RESOURCE CAPACITY HELD TO PRESERVE FUTURE STRATEGIC OPTIONS

ADAPTATION CAPACITY
= ABILITY TO CHANGE CAPABILITY, resources, policy or operating model AS CONDITIONS CHANGE

LONG-HORIZON ADAPTATION
= DELIBERATE DEVELOPMENT OF CAPABILITY FOR MATERIAL FUTURE CONDITIONS OVER EXTENDED TIME HORIZONS

PORTFOLIO REBALANCING
= GOVERNED REDISTRIBUTION OF INVESTMENT ACROSS THE PORTFOLIO

INVESTMENT REALLOCATION
= MOVEMENT OF COMMITTED OR AVAILABLE investment TO A DIFFERENT PURPOSE

INVESTMENT STOP
= AUTHORISED TERMINATION OF AN INVESTMENT

INVESTMENT PAUSE
= TEMPORARY SUSPENSION OF AN INVESTMENT

INVESTMENT PIVOT
= CONTROLLED change in investment direction BASED ON NEW EVIDENCE

PORTFOLIO LEARNING
= CONVERSION OF INVESTMENT PERFORMANCE INTO IMPROVED PORTFOLIO DECISIONS

INVESTMENT ASSURANCE
= GOVERNED EVALUATION OF WHETHER INVESTMENT IS APPROPRIATE, EFFECTIVE AND CONTROLLED

INVESTMENT OUTCOME
= OBSERVED RESULT CREATED BY AN INVESTMENT

INVESTMENT BENEFIT
= MEASURED BENEFIT ATTRIBUTABLE TO AN INVESTMENT

BENEFIT DECAY
= REDUCTION OF INVESTMENT BENEFIT OVER TIME

CAPABILITY LIFECYCLE
= PERIOD FROM CAPABILITY CREATION THROUGH OPERATION, ADAPTATION AND RETIREMENT

CAPABILITY RETIREMENT
= CONTROLLED REMOVAL OF A CAPABILITY WHEN NO LONGER JUSTIFIED

FUTURE READINESS
= DEGREE TO WHICH CURRENT CAPABILITIES AND OPTIONS MATCH PLAUSIBLE FUTURE REQUIREMENTS

RESILIENCE INVESTMENT GOVERNANCE
= GOVERNED PROCESS FOR PRIORITISING, APPROVING, executing, measuring AND rebalancing resilience investment

LONG-HORIZON LEARNING LOOP
= REPEATED CYCLE FROM FUTURE SIGNALS TO SCENARIO, investment, outcome, learning and portfolio adaptation
```

# 3. Core Principle

> **Resilience investment SHALL be treated as a portfolio of present capability, future options and adaptive capacity rather than a collection of isolated projects; investment SHALL therefore be staged, scenario-tested, value-aware and rebalanced as evidence changes.**

```text

   ↺
```

# 135. Acceptance Criteria

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-467 is accepted when:

- material resilience investment needs can be mapped to exposure, capability and scenarios;
- scenario portfolios are governed and refreshable;
- investment options include timing, staging and optionality considerations;
- no-investment and waiting options remain visible where appropriate;
- investment value, resilience return and marginal value can be assessed;
- cost of delay and reversal are visible;
- portfolio concentration and correlated dependencies can be detected;
- investment capacity and change saturation are governed;
- staged investment and tranche gates are supported;
- investments can be paused, pivoted, stopped or reallocated under controlled authority;
- benefits and benefit decay can be measured;
- investment debt and underinvestment remain visible;
- future readiness and strategic optionality are explicit investment considerations;
- long-horizon adaptation can be governed;
- scenario stress testing can influence portfolio decisions;
- AI assistance remains bounded and explainable;
- manual fallback exists;
- historical investment and portfolio decisions are reconstructable;
- negative and scenario tests prevent unsupported investment commitments and portfolio decisions.
