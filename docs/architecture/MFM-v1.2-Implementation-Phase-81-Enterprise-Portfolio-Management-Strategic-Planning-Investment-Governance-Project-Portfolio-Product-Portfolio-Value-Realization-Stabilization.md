# 1. Purpose

This document defines the eighty-first implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-80 – Enterprise Architecture, Capability Management, Business Architecture, Solution Architecture, Technology Architecture & Architecture Assurance Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish a controlled enterprise portfolio capability covering strategic planning, objectives, investment governance, portfolio prioritization, project portfolio management, product portfolio management, capability investment, demand management, resource allocation, portfolio dependencies, portfolio risk, benefits management, value realization and portfolio assurance.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must translate strategy into a governed portfolio of investments, projects, products and capability improvements, ensuring that scarce resources are directed toward the highest-value outcomes while risk, dependencies, capacity and benefits remain visible and controlled.**

# 130. Definition of Ready

A portfolio work item is Ready when:

- Strategic context is defined.
- Business need is documented.
- Expected outcome is known.
- Sponsor and owner are assigned.
- Preliminary value and risk are understood.
- Capacity implications are identified.
- Material dependencies are known.

# 132. Final Strategy Principle

> **Every material portfolio investment must have a clear relationship to strategy, objectives, capabilities or measurable organizational outcomes.**

---

# 133. Final Investment Principle

> **Investment decisions must balance strategic value, financial value, risk, capacity, dependencies and expected outcomes.**

---

# 134. Final Prioritization Principle

> **Portfolio priorities must be explicit, evidence-based and revisited when strategy, risk, capacity or expected value changes.**

---

# 135. Final Capacity Principle

> **The portfolio must not assume unlimited organizational capacity; commitments must remain aligned with realistic people, skills, funding, technology and management capacity.**

---

# 136. Final Project Principle

> **Projects are portfolio investments and must remain aligned with strategic outcomes throughout their lifecycle.**

---

# 137. Final Product Principle

> **Products must be managed as enduring value-producing capabilities with explicit lifecycle, investment, performance and ownership.**

---

# 138. Final Capability Principle

> **Capability investment should close strategically important maturity gaps and create sustainable organizational ability rather than isolated outputs.**

---

# 139. Final Benefits Principle

> **Benefits must have owners, baselines, targets and measures so that delivery activity can be connected to actual organizational outcomes.**

---

# 140. Final Value Principle

> **An investment is not successful merely because it was delivered; success requires evidence that intended outcomes and value were achieved or appropriately explained.**

---

# 141. Final Risk Principle

> **Portfolio risk must remain visible at enterprise level so that local project or product decisions do not create unmanaged systemic exposure.**

---

# 142. Final Rebalancing Principle

> **The portfolio must be capable of stopping, delaying, accelerating or reshaping investments when strategic conditions, risk, capacity or value change.**

---

# 143. Final Assurance Principle

> **Portfolio assurance must provide evidence-based confidence that investment decisions, prioritization, capacity, benefits and value realization are governed effectively.**

---

# 144. Final Integration Principle

> **Portfolio Management must integrate with Strategy, Enterprise Architecture, Financial Management, Project Management, Product Management, Risk, Service, Supplier, Data and Enterprise Assurance governance.**

---

# 145. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should manage strategy execution through a controlled portfolio lifecycle connecting strategic objectives, demand, investment, projects, products, capabilities, capacity, dependencies, risk, benefits and realized value.**
