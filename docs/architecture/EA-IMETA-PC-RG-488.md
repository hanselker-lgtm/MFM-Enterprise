# 2. Architectural Position

RG-487 established economic regime intelligence, predictive discontinuity detection, strategic interlock and bounded pre-emptive governance.

RG-488 moves from **prediction** toward **navigation of multiple plausible futures**.

```text
RG-485 ECONOMIC EQUILIBRIUM
        ↓
RG-486 SYSTEMIC ADAPTATION
        ↓
RG-487 REGIME INTELLIGENCE
        ↓
RG-488 STRATEGIC-ECONOMIC FORESIGHT
        ↓
MULTI-HORIZON SCENARIO NAVIGATION
        ↓
ADAPTIVE OPTION PORTFOLIOS
        ↓
ANTICIPATORY DECISION ORCHESTRATION
```

The architecture does not attempt to identify one inevitable future. It maintains plausible futures, identifies robust decisions, preserves options and determines when evidence or authority is sufficient for commitment.

# 3. Core Architectural Question

**How can the enterprise navigate multiple plausible economic and strategic futures, preserve option value, make timing-sensitive commitments and coordinate anticipatory decisions without allowing forecasts or autonomous systems to become a substitute for strategy?**

RG-488 treats future states, decision timing, option portfolios, strategic sensitivity and evidence thresholds as first-class architecture objects.

# 4. Governing Principle

**The enterprise SHALL prepare for multiple plausible futures rather than optimise blindly for a single forecast; it SHALL preserve strategic optionality, distinguish reversible preparation from irreversible commitment, coordinate decisions across horizons and require appropriate human authority whenever strategy, constitutional constraints or material enterprise commitments are affected.**

```text
CURRENT STATE → FUTURE LANDSCAPE → SCENARIO ENSEMBLE
→ DECISION POINTS → OPTION PORTFOLIO → TIMING ANALYSIS
→ AUTHORITY GATE → ANTICIPATORY ACTION → NEW EVIDENCE
```

# 7. Multi-Horizon Architecture

RG-488 SHALL distinguish at least:

```text
H0 NOW / EXECUTION
H1 NEAR-TERM / TACTICAL
H2 MEDIUM-TERM / STRATEGIC
H3 LONG-TERM / TRANSFORMATION
```

Exact durations SHALL be domain-specific and governed. Each horizon SHALL expose objectives, constraints, decisions, dependencies, options, commitments and uncertainties.

# 8. Cross-Horizon Interlock

```text
H3 STRATEGY
   ↓
H2 STRATEGIC OPTIONS
   ↓
H1 TACTICAL ALLOCATION
   ↓
H0 EXECUTION
   ↺
```

The interlock SHALL identify horizon collision, strategic option erosion, liquidity lock-in, capacity lock-in, resource lock-in, premature commitment and underinvestment in future capability.

# 9. Future Landscape Model

The future landscape SHALL distinguish plausible, probable, preferred, low-confidence and structurally constrained futures.

A preferred future SHALL NOT automatically be treated as the most probable future.

# 10. Scenario Navigation

Scenario navigation SHALL determine:

1. what to do now;
2. what to prepare;
3. what to defer;
4. what evidence to seek;
5. what trigger activates a future option;
6. when the decision must be revisited.

Decisions that remain valid across several scenarios SHOULD be preferred when uncertainty is high.

# 11. Robust Decision Architecture

Material decisions SHOULD be evaluated across the scenario ensemble.

```text
DECISION
 ├── SCENARIO A → OUTCOME
 ├── SCENARIO B → OUTCOME
 ├── SCENARIO C → OUTCOME
 └── SCENARIO D → OUTCOME
        ↓
ROBUSTNESS MARGIN
```

Worst-case outcomes SHALL remain visible.

# 12. Adaptive Option Portfolio

The option portfolio SHALL include combinations of:

```text
PREPARE → PROBE → HEDGE → DEFER → COMMIT → TRANSFORM
```

Options SHALL be evaluated for cost, readiness, activation window, reversibility, strategic impact, liquidity impact and expected value.

# 13. Option Readiness

Option readiness SHALL include, where material:

```text
RESOURCE READY
CAPITAL READY
AUTHORITY READY
DATA READY
TECHNOLOGY READY
SUPPLIER READY
PEOPLE READY
EXECUTION READY
```

Conceptual existence alone SHALL not constitute readiness.

# 14. Option Decay and Expiry

Option value may decline through lost time, reduced capacity, market movement, scarcity, lock-in, dependency changes or strategic change.

The architecture SHALL monitor decay and identify when options should be exercised, refreshed or retired.

# 15. Decision Timing

Decision timing SHALL compare:

```text
VALUE OF WAITING
VS
VALUE OF PREPARATION
VS
VALUE OF EARLY COMMITMENT
```

Waiting SHALL be an explicit decision when it preserves meaningful optionality.

# 16. Information Value

Additional information has value when it can materially change a decision.

The architecture SHOULD estimate:

```text
EXPECTED INFORMATION VALUE
− INFORMATION COST
− DELAY COST
```

High-value information SHOULD be acquired before irreversible commitments when the decision window permits.

# 17. Probe Actions

Probe actions SHALL be bounded experiments designed to reduce uncertainty.

Each probe SHALL define a hypothesis, expected information, cost, exposure limit, success criteria, failure criteria and decision implication.

# 18. Strategic Hedges

Strategic hedges reduce exposure across uncertain futures. Examples include diversified suppliers, staged capital, modular capacity, dual technology paths, contractual flexibility and reserve capacity.

Hedges SHALL be evaluated for both cost and option preservation.

# 19. Scenario Blind Spots

Blind spots MAY arise from shared assumptions, model consensus, historical bias, missing external dependencies, ignored weak signals or overconfidence in the current regime.

Material blind spots SHALL trigger scenario expansion or governance review.

# 20. Foresight Gap

Foresight gap is:

```text
REQUIRED FUTURE UNDERSTANDING
            −
CURRENT EVIDENCE / MODEL CAPABILITY
```

A high gap SHALL reduce confidence in irreversible commitments and MAY increase the value of preparation or information acquisition.

# 21. Strategic Optionality

Strategic optionality is an enterprise asset. It can be increased through staged commitments, modular architecture, diversified dependencies, preserved liquidity, prepared alternatives and reversible contracts.

It can be destroyed by excessive lock-in, concentrated dependencies and irreversible commitments.

# 26. Decision Governance

Material anticipatory decisions SHALL identify decision owner, strategic objective, scenario set, alternatives, timing window, reversibility, authority, constraints, expected outcomes, downside and evidence threshold.

# 27. AI and Autonomous Anticipatory Decisioning

AI MAY generate scenario ensembles, identify robust decisions, detect blind spots, estimate option decay, rank information value, propose probes, recommend timing and coordinate bounded preparations.

AI SHALL NOT change strategy, select strategic objectives, redefine constraints, eliminate inconvenient scenarios, convert preference into probability or activate material strategic commitments without authority.

# 30. Future-State Commitment Control

Before material commitment, the architecture SHALL assess scenario robustness, option loss, lock-in, reversibility, future capacity, liquidity, resilience, strategic coherence and alternatives.

Low-robustness, high-irreversibility commitments SHALL require elevated authority.

# 31. Decision Resilience

Decision resilience SHALL be tested against forecast error, scenario change, delayed information, constrained authority, resource scarcity and shock propagation.

A resilient decision architecture SHALL preserve fallback options.

# 32. Failure Handling

Failure conditions include scenario model failure, forecast failure, scenario blind spot, option readiness failure, timing-window failure, authority failure, cross-horizon conflict, strategic conflict, data conflict, model divergence, option conflict and decision deadlock.

Default response: preserve optionality, protect critical constraints and escalate when material.
