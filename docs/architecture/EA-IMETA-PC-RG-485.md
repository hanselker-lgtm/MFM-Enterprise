# 2. Architectural Position

RG-485 integrates the four economic layers developed immediately before it.

RG-481 established enterprise autonomous economics, opportunity cost and decision economics.

RG-482 established resource intelligence, capacity forecasting, scarcity, substitution and resource markets.

RG-483 established value networks, marginal value, portfolio interaction and multi-objective optimisation.

RG-484 established capital dynamics, liquidity, funding, investment flow, real options and dynamic capital allocation.

RG-485 therefore introduces the **economic equilibrium layer**.

The enterprise is no longer modelled as separate capital, resource and value systems. Those systems are treated as coupled state variables within a governed economic system.

```text
CAPITAL
   ↕
LIQUIDITY
   ↕
RESOURCES
   ↕
CAPACITY
   ↕
VALUE
   ↕
SCARCITY
   ↕
MARKETS
   ↕
STRATEGY
```

The objective is not permanent mathematical equilibrium. The objective is **controlled, explainable and resilient movement around a governed equilibrium band**.

# 3. Core Principle

> **Enterprise economic balance SHALL be continuously observable and governable across capital, liquidity, resources, capacity, value, scarcity and strategic demand. # 4. Architectural Principles

1. Economic equilibrium is a governed operating condition, not an autonomous objective above strategy.
2. Liquidity is both a continuity requirement and an option for future decisions.
3. Capital, resource and value states SHALL be modelled separately before being optimised jointly.
4. Cross-market effects SHALL be visible before material clearing or rebalancing.
5. Local economic optimisation SHALL not create unacceptable enterprise-wide disequilibrium.
6. Hard constraints SHALL dominate optimisation.
7. Autonomous balancing SHALL operate inside explicit envelopes.
8. Economic resilience SHALL be treated as a value-bearing constraint.
9. Uncertainty SHALL reduce autonomous decision scope when material.
10. Economic recovery SHALL favour state integrity before optimisation speed.

# 5. Core Definitions

### Economic Equilibrium
A governed state in which capital, resources, capacity, liquidity, value and strategic demand remain within defined operating envelopes.

### Economic Disequilibrium
A material divergence between supply, demand, capital availability, value requirements, liquidity or strategic objectives.

### Economic Imbalance
A measurable condition in which one or more economic variables move outside their target or safe range.

### Capital-Resource Equilibrium
Balance between deployable capital and the resources and capacity that capital can effectively mobilise.

### Value-Resource Equilibrium
Balance between resource consumption and the value that those resources can sustainably create.

### Liquidity-Optionality Trade-off
Economic relationship between preserving liquid capacity and deploying capital to create value.

### Cross-Market Clearing
Governed clearing of interacting capital, resource and capacity supply-demand signals.

### Economic State
Authoritative representation of the current enterprise economic condition.

### Economic State Vector
Structured representation of material capital, resource, liquidity, value, risk and capacity variables.

### Economic Pressure
Measured pressure created by scarcity, demand, funding constraints or value opportunities.

### Economic Temperature
Composite indicator of enterprise economic stress or expansion.

### Equilibrium Band
Approved range within which an economic variable is considered balanced.

### Equilibrium Point
Specific state at which defined supply, demand and constraint relationships are balanced.

### Disequilibrium Gradient
Magnitude and direction of divergence from an approved equilibrium band.

### Rebalancing Trigger
Condition that causes an economic rebalancing process to begin.

### Rebalancing Envelope
Bounded range within which automated rebalancing may operate.

### Economic Safe State
Conservative operating condition entered when economic intelligence or controls become unreliable.

### Economic Circuit Breaker
Control that suspends or constrains autonomous economic activity during abnormal conditions.

### Economic Recovery State
Controlled state used to restore economic balance after disruption.

### Simultaneous Scarcity
Condition in which multiple critical resources, capacities or capital pools are scarce at the same time.

### Multi-Resource Constraint
Constraint created by the combined availability of several resources.

### Cross-Market Effect
Effect in one economic market caused by activity in another market.

### Market Coupling
Degree to which two or more markets influence one another.

### Market Decoupling
Designed reduction of dependency between economic markets.

### Economic Substitution
Replacement of one economic input with another feasible input.

### Liquidity Optionality
Future decision capacity preserved by maintaining sufficient liquidity.

### Economic Slack
Uncommitted capacity, capital or resource availability above protected requirements.

### Economic Reserve
Protected economic capacity unavailable for ordinary optimisation.

### Equilibrium Margin
Distance between the current economic state and a critical constraint.

### Rebalancing Velocity
Rate at which the enterprise can restore economic balance.

### Economic Friction
Cost, delay or loss associated with changing an economic allocation.

### Economic Inertia
Resistance to changing existing allocations despite changing conditions.

### Economic Momentum
Direction and rate of change of material economic conditions.

### Systemic Economic Risk
Risk that a common dependency creates simultaneous effects across multiple economic domains.

### Economic Contagion
Propagation of an economic disturbance from one domain to another.

### Economic Shock Absorption

# 9. Equilibrium Bands

Every critical economic variable SHOULD use governed operating bands:

```text
GREEN       = NORMAL EQUILIBRIUM
AMBER       = DEVELOPING IMBALANCE
RED         = MATERIAL DISEQUILIBRIUM
CRITICAL    = PROTECTED CONSTRAINT / SAFE STATE
```

The bands SHALL not be interpreted as generic risk scores. Each band SHALL correspond to defined response authority and operational behaviour.

# 10. Cross-Domain Equilibrium

The architecture SHALL evaluate at least five relationships:

```text
CAPITAL ↔ RESOURCE
RESOURCE ↔ VALUE
CAPITAL ↔ VALUE
LIQUIDITY ↔ OPTIONALITY
```

```text
CAPITAL ↔ LIQUIDITY

```

The enterprise SHALL avoid treating any one relationship as sufficient to explain the total economic state.

# 11. Cross-Market Clearing

Capital and resource markets MAY be coupled through a governed clearing layer.

```text
CAPITAL SUPPLY
      ↓
CAPITAL DEMAND ──────┐
                     ↓
              CROSS-MARKET
                CLEARING
                     ↑
RESOURCE SUPPLY ─────┘
      ↑

```

Cross-market clearing SHALL preserve:

- liquidity floors;
- strategic reserves;
- resource capacity floors;
- security constraints;
- resilience constraints;
- authority limits;
- fairness rules where applicable.

A market price SHALL never become an implicit authority override.

# 12. Simultaneous Scarcity

When capital, resources and capacity are simultaneously scarce, the enterprise SHALL identify:

1. the binding constraint;
2. secondary constraints;
3. substitution options;
4. affected value streams;
5. liquidity impact;
6. strategic impact;
7. resilience impact;
8. time to recovery.

The system SHOULD avoid solving one scarcity by creating a more severe scarcity elsewhere.

# 13. Liquidity-Optionality Optimisation

Liquidity SHALL be evaluated as a future decision capability.

```text
MORE LIQUIDITY
      ↓
MORE OPTION VALUE
      ↓
LESS IMMEDIATE DEPLOYMENT

MORE DEPLOYMENT
      ↓
LESS LIQUIDITY
      ↓
POTENTIALLY MORE CURRENT VALUE
```

The architecture SHALL optimise this trade-off explicitly rather than treating unallocated liquidity as automatically inefficient.

# 15. Economic Safe States

A safe state SHALL be entered when one or more of the following occur:

- authoritative economic state is unavailable;
- cross-market clearing becomes inconsistent;
- liquidity confidence falls below threshold;
- material constraint conflict cannot be resolved;
- autonomous agent behaviour becomes anomalous;
- market integrity is compromised;
- reconciliation fails;
- model confidence becomes insufficient for the permitted authority level.

Safe state behaviour SHALL protect continuity, liquidity, strategic reserves and critical capacity.

# 16. Economic Circuit Breakers

Circuit breakers SHALL be capable of:

- suspending autonomous reallocation;
- freezing market clearing;
- protecting liquidity reserves;
- protecting critical resource capacity;
- forcing human approval;
- switching to conservative allocation;
- preserving the last authoritative state;
- initiating reconciliation.

Every circuit breaker SHALL have activation criteria, authority, logging and recovery criteria.
