# 1. Purpose

This document defines the ninety-fifth implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-94 – Cybersecurity Operations, Threat Management, Vulnerability, Security Monitoring, Detection, Response & Security Assurance Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish a controlled privacy and data protection capability covering privacy governance, data protection governance, personal data inventory, processing activities, lawful basis, purpose limitation, data minimization, consent governance, data subject rights, information rights requests, privacy impact assessments, data protection impact assessments, privacy by design, privacy by default, data sharing governance, international data transfers, retention and deletion alignment, privacy incidents, data breaches, privacy risk and privacy assurance.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must ensure that personal and privacy-relevant information is collected, used, shared, retained and deleted for legitimate and controlled purposes, with appropriate rights, transparency, security, accountability and assurance throughout its lifecycle.**

# 136. Definition of Ready

A privacy work item is Ready when:

- The processing purpose is defined.
- Personal data and data subject categories are identified.
- The responsible owner is known.
- The applicable lawful basis is identified or under assessment.
- Privacy risks and requirements are understood.
- Rights, transparency, retention and sharing considerations are identified.
- Security and records requirements are understood.

# 138. Final Purpose Principle

> **Personal data must be processed for defined and legitimate purposes and not used beyond those purposes without an appropriate basis and governance decision.**

---

# 139. Final Minimization Principle

> **MFM should collect and use no more personal data than is reasonably necessary for the defined purpose.**

---

# 140. Final Transparency Principle

> **Individuals should receive clear and appropriate information about material processing of their personal data.**

---

# 141. Final Consent Principle

> **Where consent is relied upon, consent must be appropriately obtained, evidenced, managed and capable of withdrawal.**

---

# 142. Final Rights Principle

> **Applicable information rights must be supported through controlled, timely and traceable processes.**

---

# 143. Final Design Principle

> **Privacy requirements should be incorporated into process, service, application and information design rather than added only after implementation.**

---

# 144. Final Sharing Principle

> **Personal data sharing must have a defined purpose, appropriate legal and governance basis, controlled recipients and proportionate security and retention controls.**

---

# 145. Final Transfer Principle

> **International transfers must be identified, assessed and governed according to applicable requirements and risk.**

---

# 146. Final Retention Principle

> **Personal data should not be retained longer than necessary for its defined purpose and applicable obligations.**

---

# 147. Final Incident Principle

> **Privacy incidents and personal data breaches must be assessed promptly, managed through a controlled lifecycle and escalated or notified where required.**

---

# 148. Final Assurance Principle

> **Privacy assurance must provide evidence-based confidence that personal data processing and associated controls comply with defined requirements and support individual rights.**

---

# 149. Final Integration Principle

> **Privacy Governance must integrate with Security, Data Governance, Identity, Records Management, Architecture, Procurement, Service Management, Risk, Compliance and Enterprise Assurance governance.**

---

# 150. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should manage privacy through a controlled lifecycle connecting processing purposes, data inventories, lawful bases, consent, transparency, rights, DPIAs, sharing, transfers, retention, deletion, incidents and continuous assurance.**
