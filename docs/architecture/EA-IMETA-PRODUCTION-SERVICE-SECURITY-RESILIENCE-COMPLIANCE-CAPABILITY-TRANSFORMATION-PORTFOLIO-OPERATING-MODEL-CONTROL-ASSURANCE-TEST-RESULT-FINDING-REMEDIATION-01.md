# 1. PURPOSE

Establish the authoritative remediation-management architecture for findings arising from portfolio operating-model assurance, including corrective actions, preventive actions, ownership, milestones, dependencies, evidence, risk reduction, validation, retesting, escalation and closure.

# 2. REMEDIATION PRINCIPLE

EA-IMETA SHALL TREAT REMEDIATION AS A CONTROLLED TRANSFORMATION ACTIVITY THAT RESTORES OR IMPROVES CONTROL EFFECTIVENESS, REDUCES RISK, PREVENTS RECURRENCE AND PRODUCES VERIFIABLE EVIDENCE.

# 3. SCOPE

The remediation model covers finding remediation, corrective action, preventive action, root-cause treatment, technical fixes, process changes, governance changes, security remediation, resilience improvements, data correction, AI/agent controls, supplier actions and systemic transformation.

# 4. ACCOUNTABILITY

Every material remediation shall have one accountable owner, defined actions, target dates, dependencies, evidence requirements, acceptance criteria and escalation conditions.

# 5. REMEDIATION LIFECYCLE

FINDING → ROOT CAUSE → PLAN → ASSIGN → EXECUTE → EVIDENCE → VALIDATE → RETEST → ACCEPT → CLOSE.

# 6. CORRECTIVE VS PREVENTIVE

Corrective action addresses the observed deficiency; preventive action addresses the conditions that could cause recurrence.

# 7. ROOT CAUSE ALIGNMENT

Remediation shall address the validated root cause rather than merely treating symptoms unless containment is explicitly required as an immediate action.

# 8. RISK REDUCTION

Material remediation shall state the expected risk reduction and how that reduction will be validated.

# 9. DEPENDENCIES

Remediation dependencies shall be explicitly identified, owned and managed when delivery depends on other initiatives, suppliers, architecture, technology, data or organizational change.

# 10. EVIDENCE

Remediation evidence shall demonstrate implementation, configuration, process adoption, control operation or other objective proof appropriate to the action.

# 11. VALIDATION

Completion status is not sufficient evidence of remediation effectiveness. Validation shall establish whether the intended outcome was achieved.

# 12. RETEST

Where remediation restores a tested control, retesting shall be linked to the original finding and preserve historical traceability.

# 13. OVERDUE REMEDIATION

Overdue material remediation shall be visible, risk-assessed and escalated according to governance thresholds.

# 14. RISK ACCEPTANCE

Where remediation is not proportionate or cannot be completed within the required period, authorized residual-risk acceptance may be used instead of silently extending the due date.

# 15. AI REMEDIATION

AI may assist with root-cause analysis, action design, prioritization or evidence review, but remediation decisions remain governed and accountable.

# 16. AGENT REMEDIATION

Agent-driven remediation shall have explicit authority, action boundaries, approval gates, rollback or stop conditions and complete auditability.

# 17. CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

Remediation outcomes shall feed recurring-finding analysis, control improvement, architecture improvement, security/resilience learning and operating-model maturity.
