# 2. Architectural Position

RG-492 established execution-value realisation, delivery economics, throughput optimisation, outcome feedback and autonomous value-flow governance.

RG-493 moves the architecture from individual value flows to the **enterprise outcome network**: outcomes interact, reinforce, constrain and sometimes compete with one another. Benefits may depend on several delivered capabilities, while one capability may contribute to many benefits.

```text
RG-491 EXECUTION BALANCE
        ↓
RG-492 VALUE REALISATION
        ↓
RG-493 VALUE-OUTCOME NETWORKS
        ↓
BENEFIT REALISATION ORCHESTRATION
        ↓
CROSS-VALUESTREAM OPTIMISATION
        ↓
VALUE RESILIENCE
        ↓
AUTONOMIC OUTCOME GOVERNANCE
```

# 3. Core Architectural Question

**How can the enterprise govern an interconnected network of outcomes and benefits so that delivery across multiple value streams converges on durable enterprise value, while dependencies, conflicts, benefit leakage, resilience exposure and autonomous optimisation remain visible and controlled?**

RG-493 therefore treats outcomes, benefits, value dependencies, benefit chains, cross-value-stream interactions and outcome resilience as first-class architectural objects.

# 4. Governing Principle

**An enterprise outcome SHALL be governed in relation to the broader outcome network in which it participates. Benefit realisation SHALL be evidence-based, cross-value-stream dependencies SHALL be explicit, and optimisation SHALL preserve strategic coherence, resilience, security, liquidity and human authority. Autonomous outcome governance SHALL coordinate only within explicit policy and authority envelopes.**

```text
STRATEGIC INTENT
      ↓
OUTCOME NETWORK
      ↓
BENEFIT CHAIN
      ↓
VALUE STREAMS
      ↓
REALISATION
      ↓
RESILIENCE
      ↓
FEEDBACK
      ↓
ENTERPRISE REBALANCING
```

# 7. Outcome Network Architecture

The outcome network SHALL connect enterprise intent to measurable outcomes and benefits.

```text
STRATEGY
   ↓
OBJECTIVES
   ↓
OUTCOME A ─────→ BENEFIT A
   ↓                 ↓
OUTCOME B ─────→ BENEFIT B
   ↓                 ↓
OUTCOME C ─────→ ENTERPRISE VALUE
```

The graph SHALL represent both positive and negative interactions where material.

# 8. Outcome Relationship Types

| Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ENABLES | One outcome enables another |
| REQUIRES | One outcome requires another |
| REINFORCES | One outcome increases another's effectiveness |
| CONSTRAINS | One outcome limits another |
| CONFLICTS | Outcomes compete or are incompatible |
| PRECEDES | One outcome must occur first |
| AMPLIFIES | One outcome increases downstream impact |
| SUBSTITUTES | One outcome can replace another path |
| CASCADES | One outcome creates downstream effects |

# 9. Benefit Chain Architecture

A material benefit SHALL be traceable through:

```text
CAPABILITY
 ↓
OUTCOME
 ↓
ADOPTION
 ↓
BENEFIT
 ↓
ENTERPRISE VALUE
```

The chain SHALL identify ownership, evidence, assumptions and dependencies.

# 10. Benefit Realisation Orchestration

Benefit realisation often requires several outcomes to converge.

```text
OUTCOME A ─┐
OUTCOME B ─┼→ BENEFIT X
OUTCOME C ─┘
```

The benefit SHALL not be declared fully realised until material prerequisites are satisfied.

# 11. Benefit Ownership

Each material benefit SHALL have an accountable owner.

The owner SHALL be responsible for:

- target;
- evidence;
- realisation plan;
- dependency management;
- variance;
- recovery;
- rebaseline requests.

# 12. Benefit Evidence

Benefit claims SHALL be supported by evidence appropriate to the claim.

Evidence SHOULD include:

- operational measures;
- financial measures;
- adoption measures;
- quality measures;
- customer or stakeholder evidence;
- resilience measures.

# 13. Benefit Confidence

Benefit measurements SHALL retain confidence.

```text
MEASURE
 ↓
EVIDENCE QUALITY
 ↓
ATTRIBUTION CONFIDENCE
 ↓
BENEFIT CONFIDENCE
```

Low-confidence benefits SHALL not automatically justify aggressive autonomous reallocation.

# 14. Benefit Gap

```text
TARGET BENEFIT − REALISED BENEFIT = BENEFIT GAP
```

The gap SHALL be decomposed into adoption, quality, timing, dependency, execution, cost and external-condition effects where material.

# 15. Benefit Leakage

Benefit leakage may arise from:

- incomplete adoption;
- unused capability;
- process fragmentation;
- duplicated work;
- poor transition;
- quality defects;
- delayed decisions;
- dependency failure;
- excessive sustaining cost.

# 16. Benefit Decay

Benefits SHALL be monitored for decay caused by:

- market change;
- technology change;
- user behaviour;
- competitive response;
- capability degradation;
- maintenance burden;
- strategic change.

# 17. Benefit Durability

Durability SHALL distinguish temporary benefit from sustained benefit.

A benefit that disappears after initial delivery SHALL not be treated as permanently realised.

# 18. Cross-Value-Stream Dependencies

Cross-stream dependencies SHALL be explicit.

```text
VALUE STREAM A ───┐
                  ├→ OUTCOME X
VALUE STREAM B ───┘
```

Dependencies SHALL expose timing, capacity, ownership and failure consequences.

# 19. Cross-Value-Stream Synergy

Synergy exists when coordinated value streams produce greater value than isolated optimisation.

Synergy claims SHALL be evidence-based and SHALL not be assumed merely because streams are related.

# 20. Cross-Value-Stream Conflict

Conflict SHALL be detected where one value stream's optimisation degrades another's outcomes, resilience or strategic contribution.

Enterprise governance SHALL arbitrate material conflicts.

# 21. Cross-Value-Stream Optimisation

Optimisation SHALL consider:

```text
STREAM A VALUE
+
STREAM B VALUE
+
SYNERGY
−
CONFLICT
−
SHARED COST
−
RESILIENCE EXPOSURE
=
ENTERPRISE VALUE EFFECT
```

# 22. Outcome Critical Paths

Critical paths SHALL identify dependencies whose failure materially delays or reduces outcome realisation.

Critical paths SHALL be visible across value-stream boundaries.

# 23. Benefit Critical Paths

Benefit critical paths SHALL identify all material prerequisites for benefit realisation, including adoption and operational transition.

# 24. Outcome Bottlenecks

Outcome bottlenecks may occur in:

- delivery;
- adoption;
- approval;
- integration;
- data;
- operations;
- governance.

The architecture SHALL not restrict bottleneck analysis to delivery.

# 25. Benefit Choke Points

A benefit choke point is a dependency through which multiple benefits depend.

Choke points SHALL have contingency and recovery options where critical.

# 26. Outcome Cascades

```text
OUTCOME A
 ↓
OUTCOME B
 ↓
BENEFIT C
 ↓
VALUE D
```

Material cascades SHALL be modelled before major changes to upstream outcomes.

# 27. Outcome Amplification

Positive amplification SHALL be distinguished from uncontrolled exposure.

Amplification SHALL be tested for:

- capacity effects;
- resilience effects;
- concentration;
- reversibility.

# 28. Outcome Substitution

Alternative outcome paths MAY preserve strategic intent when a preferred path fails.

Substitution SHALL compare value, cost, timing, resilience and dependencies.

# 29. Outcome Resilience

Critical outcomes SHALL have defined resilience expectations.

Resilience SHALL consider:

- disruption tolerance;
- alternative paths;
- recovery time;
- dependency concentration;
- resource availability.

# 30. Benefit Resilience

Benefits SHALL be assessed for continued realisation under:

- supply disruption;
- adoption changes;
- technology failure;
- market change;
- resource scarcity.

# 31. Value Resilience

Value resilience SHALL connect outcome resilience to enterprise economic resilience.

High-value outcomes with low resilience SHALL receive explicit governance attention.

# 32. Outcome Fragility

Fragility SHALL identify outcomes highly sensitive to a small number of dependencies.

Fragile outcomes SHALL not be mistaken for efficient outcomes.

# 33. Common-Cause Failure

Common-cause failures SHALL be modelled where multiple outcomes depend on:

- one supplier;
- one platform;
- one capability;
- one data source;
- one authority;
- one infrastructure component.

# 34. Systemic Outcome Stress

Systemic stress occurs when multiple outcome paths approach simultaneous constraint.

```text
DEPENDENCY STRESS
      +
CAPACITY STRESS
      +
ADOPTION STRESS
      +
RESOURCE STRESS
      ↓
SYSTEMIC OUTCOME STRESS
```

# 35. Benefit Recovery

Recovery SHALL identify:

- lost benefit;
- root cause;
- recovery path;
- recovery cost;
- recovery window;
- expected recovered benefit;
- authority.

# 36. Benefit Recovery Economics

```text
RECOVERABLE BENEFIT
−
RECOVERY COST
−
RISK PREMIUM
=
NET RECOVERY VALUE
```

Recovery should proceed when the expected net value is positive and compatible with strategic constraints.

# 37. Outcome Optionality

Outcome optionality preserves alternative paths.

Optimisation SHALL not eliminate alternative paths merely to improve short-term efficiency without explicit authority.

# 38. Benefit Optionality

Benefit optionality allows future benefit paths to remain available.

This SHALL be considered in reallocation decisions.

# 39. Timing and Benefit Windows

Some benefits have time-sensitive windows.

```text
BENEFIT WINDOW
|---------|
   ↑
REALISATION
```

Late realisation may materially reduce value even when delivery quality remains high.

# 40. Benefit Debt

Benefit debt SHALL identify benefits that remain below target because enabling work, adoption or remediation is incomplete.

# 41. Outcome Debt

Outcome debt SHALL identify delivered capability that has not yet produced the intended outcome.

# 42. Measurement Debt

Measurement debt SHALL identify material uncertainty caused by inadequate measurement.

Measurement debt SHALL reduce confidence in autonomous optimisation.

# 43. Benefit Portfolio Balance

The enterprise SHALL monitor benefit concentration, dependency concentration, timing concentration and resilience exposure.

# 44. Outcome Portfolio Rebalancing

Outcome rebalancing MAY shift capacity between outcomes when evidence changes.

Rebalancing SHALL preserve critical outcomes and strategic commitments.

# 45. Benefit Portfolio Rebalancing

Benefit rebalancing SHALL consider:

- realised benefit;
- remaining opportunity;
- recovery potential;
- strategic importance;
- resilience;
- optionality;
- capacity consumption.
