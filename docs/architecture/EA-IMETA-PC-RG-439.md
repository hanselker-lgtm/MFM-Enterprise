# 2. Purpose

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-439 establishes the higher-order assurance and adaptive-improvement layer above steady-state monitoring.

RG-438 establishes continuous monitoring, drift detection, regression control and long-term sustainability management.

RG-439 establishes **how the organisation determines whether a sustainable state remains resilient, whether its controls remain fit for purpose, whether improvement opportunities are justified, and whether changes to the target state can be introduced without weakening governance or invalidating previous assurance**.

The architecture SHALL distinguish:

```text
SUSTAINABILITY ASSURANCE
= GOVERNED CHALLENGE OF WHETHER A SUSTAINABLE STATE REMAINS EFFECTIVE, CONTROLLED AND SUPPORTED BY SUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

RESILIENCE
= CAPABILITY OF A SUSTAINABLE STATE TO ABSORB, ADAPT TO AND RECOVER FROM RELEVANT DISRUPTION WHILE PRESERVING REQUIRED OUTCOMES

RESILIENCE TEST
= CONTROLLED TEST OF THE STATE'S ABILITY TO WITHSTAND OR RECOVER FROM A DEFINED STRESSOR

CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
= GOVERNED CHANGE INTENDED TO IMPROVE PERFORMANCE, CONTROL, RESILIENCE, EFFICIENCY OR VALUE WITHOUT COMPROMISING REQUIRED CONDITIONS

IMPROVEMENT SIGNAL
= EVIDENCE THAT THE CURRENT STATE MAY BE IMPROVED OR THAT ITS EXISTING DESIGN IS NO LONGER OPTIMAL

ASSURANCE GAP
= DIFFERENCE BETWEEN REQUIRED ASSURANCE AND AVAILABLE ASSURANCE EVIDENCE

RESILIENCE GAP
= DIFFERENCE BETWEEN REQUIRED RESILIENCE AND OBSERVED OR TESTED RESILIENCE

IMPROVEMENT DEBT
= APPROVED OR MATERIAL IMPROVEMENTS THAT REMAIN UNADDRESSED

ADAPTIVE CHANGE
= GOVERNED CHANGE TO A SUSTAINABLE STATE IN RESPONSE TO NEW EVIDENCE, CONDITIONS OR OPPORTUNITY

REVALIDATION
= CONTROLLED CONFIRMATION THAT A CHANGED STATE STILL SATISFIES ITS REQUIRED OUTCOME, CONTROL, RISK AND SUSTAINABILITY CRITERIA
```

---

# 3. Core Principle

> **A sustainable state must not only remain stable; it must remain capable of resisting relevant disruption and adapting to justified change without losing its verified outcome, control integrity or accountability.**

The governing chain is:

```text
SUSTAINABLE STATE
      ↓
ASSURANCE
      ↓
RESILIENCE ASSESSMENT
      ↓
STRESS / SIGNAL
      ↓
IMPROVEMENT OR CONTROL NEED
      ↓
GOVERNED CHANGE
      ↓
VALIDATION
      ↓
REVERIFICATION
      ↓
UPDATED SUSTAINABLE STATE
```

# 248. Acceptance Criteria

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-439 is accepted when:

- sustainability assurance has explicit scope, criteria, evidence and independence;
- assurance opinions distinguish supported conclusions from evidence limitations;
- assurance debt and coverage gaps are measurable;
- sustainable-state resilience is explicitly defined and tested;
- critical capabilities and dependencies are identified;
- resilience tolerances and recovery objectives are governed;
- stress testing is risk-informed and controlled;
- `NOT TESTED ≠ PASS`;
- resilience gaps create governed responses;
- continuous improvement is distinguished from correction and reintervention;
- improvement opportunities have evidence, ownership, value and risk;
- improvement debt and change saturation are measurable;
- pilots, rollback and validation can be governed;
- material target-state changes require validation and revalidation;
- previous sustainable states remain historically traceable;
- lessons are distinguished from findings and can be reused;
- control effectiveness and control obsolescence can be reassessed;
- resilience debt is measurable;
- AI-assisted assurance, resilience and improvement remain explainable and non-authoritative for material decisions;
- portfolio assurance and resilience are supported;
- closed-loop governance returns the system from assurance and improvement to verified sustainability;
- manual fallback and historical reconstruction are supported;
- negative tests prevent unsupported assurance, resilience and target-state claims.

# 250. Governing Principle

> **The governance system must govern itself: assurance must be assured, resilience must be tested, improvement must be evidenced, and changes to the sustainable state must remain traceable to validated learning rather than unmanaged preference.**

The PC-RG architecture SHALL therefore operate as a self-evaluating, evidence-driven and continuously improving governance system in which sustainable states, assurance mechanisms, resilience capabilities and improvement processes are themselves subject to controlled review, challenge and renewal.
