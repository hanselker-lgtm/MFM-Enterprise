# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the ninety-second document in the MFM v1.2 Steady-State series.

It follows MFM v1.2-Steady-State-91 – Enterprise Asset Management, Configuration Management, Hardware, Software, Cloud Resources & Technology Lifecycle Governance.

The purpose is to establish the permanent enterprise operating model for service management governance, service strategy, service ownership, service portfolio, service catalog, business services, technology services, service levels, service level agreements, operational level agreements, underpinning agreements, service requests, incidents, major incidents, problems, known errors, service monitoring, event coordination, service availability, service capacity, service continuity coordination, service quality, service reporting, service improvement, service assurance, service metrics, dashboards, maturity and continual enterprise service management improvement.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must provide reliable, measurable and continuously improving services through accountable service ownership, defined service levels, controlled incident and request management, effective problem management and evidence-based service assurance.**

# 3. Service Governance Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Establish clear accountability, decision rights, service ownership and operational governance for enterprise services.**

# 4. Service Strategy Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Ensure services are designed and operated to meet business outcomes, customer needs, risk requirements and strategic priorities.**

# 5. Service Catalog Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Provide an authoritative and understandable representation of available services, ownership, service levels, dependencies and support arrangements.**

# 6. Service Level Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Define measurable service commitments and performance expectations aligned with business requirements.**

# 7. Incident Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Restore normal service as quickly as practical while minimizing business impact.**

# 8. Problem Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Identify and address underlying causes of recurring or significant incidents and service failures.**

# 9. Service Assurance Objective

The primary objective is:

> **Provide reliable evidence that services meet agreed requirements and operate within defined performance, risk and resilience expectations.**

# 103. Definition of Ready

A service, catalog entry, SLA, service request, incident, major incident, problem, known error, improvement, exception, remediation or assurance review is Ready when scope, owner, customer or business impact, priority, dependencies, service requirements, decision authority, acceptance criteria and evidence requirements are defined.

# 104. Definition of Done

```text
Requirement Identified
        ↓

        ↓
Service Action Completed
        ↓
Operational / Business / Security / Risk / Continuity Validation Completed where Required
        ↓
Service Records Updated
        ↓
Evidence Captured
        ↓
Findings / Exceptions Addressed
        ↓
Outcome Accepted
```

# 105. Final Service Governance Principle

> **MFM must provide reliable, measurable and continuously improving services through accountable service ownership, defined service levels, controlled incident and request management, effective problem management and evidence-based service assurance.**

# 106. Final Service Ownership Principle

> **Every material service must have clear ownership, defined customers or stakeholders, support arrangements, dependencies and measurable service expectations.**

# 107. Final Catalog Principle

> **The service catalog must provide an authoritative representation of available services, ownership, support, service levels and dependencies.**

# 108. Final SLA Principle

> **Service levels must translate business requirements into measurable, monitored and reportable commitments.**

# 109. Final Incident Principle

> **Incidents must be prioritized according to business impact and urgency and managed to restore service as quickly as practical.**

# 110. Final Major Incident Principle

> **Major incidents require clear leadership, coordinated technical response, controlled communication, decision logging and post-incident learning.**

# 111. Final Problem Principle

> **Problem management must address underlying causes of recurring or significant service failures rather than relying solely on incident resolution.**

# 112. Final Monitoring Principle

> **Critical services must be monitored using appropriate availability, performance, capacity and risk indicators.**

# 113. Final Improvement Principle

> **Service performance, customer feedback, incidents, problems, risks and assurance results must continuously improve service quality and resilience.**

# 114. Final Assurance Principle

> **Material service commitments and operational controls must be supported by reliable evidence and periodically assured.**

# 115. Final Integration Principle

> **Service Management must integrate with Asset Management, Configuration Management, Enterprise Architecture, Applications, Infrastructure, Cloud, Cybersecurity, Identity, Data, Finance, Procurement, Suppliers, Risk, Compliance, Legal and Business Continuity.**

# 116. Final Steady-State Service Principle

> **MFM must provide reliable, measurable and continuously improving services through accountable service ownership, defined service levels, controlled incident and request management, effective problem management and evidence-based service assurance.**
