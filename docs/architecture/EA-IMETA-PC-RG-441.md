# 2. Purpose

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-441 establishes the cross-domain integration layer above governance maturity, assurance effectiveness and systemic resilience.

RG-440 establishes how governance capability itself is measured and improved.

RG-441 establishes **how multiple governance domains operate together when risks, controls, dependencies, decisions, resources, outcomes or interventions cross organisational boundaries**.

The architecture SHALL distinguish:

```text
DOMAIN GOVERNANCE
= GOVERNANCE WITHIN A DEFINED BUSINESS, TECHNICAL OR FUNCTIONAL BOUNDARY

CROSS-DOMAIN GOVERNANCE
= COORDINATED GOVERNANCE ACROSS TWO OR MORE DOMAINS

INTEGRATION
= CONTROLLED ALIGNMENT OF OBJECTIVES, DECISIONS, CONTROLS, DATA, RESOURCES AND ACCOUNTABILITY

CROSS-DOMAIN DEPENDENCY
= CONDITION WHERE ONE DOMAIN RELIES ON ANOTHER FOR OUTCOME, CONTROL, RESOURCE, DATA OR CAPABILITY

CROSS-DOMAIN CONFLICT
= CONDITION WHERE OBJECTIVES, POLICIES, CONTROLS, PRIORITIES OR DECISIONS OF DIFFERENT DOMAINS ARE INCOMPATIBLE OR COMPETE

SYSTEMIC CONDITION
= CONDITION WHOSE CAUSE, EFFECT OR REQUIRED RESPONSE EXTENDS ACROSS MULTIPLE DOMAINS

SYSTEMIC INTERVENTION
= COORDINATED ACTION ACROSS MULTIPLE DOMAINS TO ADDRESS A SYSTEMIC CONDITION

INTEGRATION FAILURE
= FAILURE TO COORDINATE MATERIAL CROSS-DOMAIN CONDITIONS

CONFLICT RESOLUTION
= GOVERNED PROCESS FOR REACHING AN AUTHORISED AND TRACEABLE DECISION WHEN DOMAIN INTERESTS OR REQUIREMENTS CONFLICT

SYSTEMIC OWNER
= ACCOUNTABLE AUTHORITY FOR COORDINATING A SYSTEMIC CONDITION

ENTERPRISE BASELINE
= COMMON REFERENCE STATE USED TO COMPARE CROSS-DOMAIN PERFORMANCE AND RISK

CROSS-DOMAIN CONTROL
= CONTROL WHOSE EFFECTIVENESS DEPENDS ON MORE THAN ONE DOMAIN

INTEGRATED ASSURANCE
= ASSURANCE THAT EVALUATES CROSS-DOMAIN CONDITIONS RATHER THAN ONLY INDIVIDUAL DOMAIN PERFORMANCE

SYSTEMIC REVALIDATION
= GOVERNED CONFIRMATION THAT A COORDINATED intervention achieved the intended enterprise-level condition
```

# 3. Core Principle

> **A governance system is only as strong as its weakest material cross-domain dependency; local optimisation cannot be allowed to conceal enterprise-level risk, control conflict or systemic failure.**

The governing chain is:

```text
DOMAIN CONDITIONS
      ↓
CROSS-DOMAIN DEPENDENCIES
      ↓
INTEGRATION
      ↓
CONFLICT / SYSTEMIC SIGNAL
      ↓
SYSTEMIC ANALYSIS
      ↓
COORDINATED DECISION
      ↓
SYSTEMIC INTERVENTION
      ↓
INTEGRATED VERIFICATION
      ↓
ENTERPRISE SUSTAINABILITY
```

# 230. Acceptance Criteria

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-441 is accepted when:

- domain boundaries and ownership are explicit;
- cross-domain dependencies are identified, owned and monitored;
- shared dependencies and concentration risk are visible;
- cross-domain interfaces have governed contracts;
- shared data definitions and lineage are controlled;
- policy conflicts can be detected and resolved;
- cross-domain control chains are traceable;
- local optimisation cannot silently conceal enterprise risk;
- cross-domain risks are aggregated without double-counting or under-counting;
- material conflicts have explicit authority and rationale;
- systemic conditions are evidence-based;
- systemic conditions are distinguished from coincidental cross-domain events;
- systemic interventions have defined scope, ownership, resources and authority;
- integrated milestones and critical paths are supported;
- integrated assurance covers cross-domain conditions;
- systemic resilience and recovery can be tested;
- cross-domain learning is transferred and retained;
- shared capability and knowledge dependencies are governed;
- enterprise outcomes and benefits remain integrated;
- systemic regression can re-enter the governance lifecycle;
- cross-domain governance debt is visible;
- AI-assisted integration remains explainable and non-authoritative for material decisions;
- historical dependencies, decisions, policies and baselines remain traceable;
- manual fallback exists;
- negative tests prevent unsupported systemic and enterprise-level claims.

# 232. Governing Principle

> **Enterprise governance must integrate domains without erasing their accountability, coordinate dependencies without obscuring ownership, resolve conflicts through explicit authority, and intervene systemically only where evidence demonstrates that local correction is insufficient.**

The PC-RG architecture SHALL therefore treat cross-domain integration as a governed capability in its own right, ensuring that enterprise outcomes, dependencies, risks, controls, resources, decisions and interventions remain coherent across boundaries.
