# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this standard is to establish the Enterprise Incident Response & Digital Forensics Architecture governing the identification, containment, investigation, recovery and continuous improvement of cybersecurity incidents across the MFM Enterprise Platform.

The Enterprise Incident Response capability shall

- minimize business disruption
- reduce cyber risk
- coordinate Enterprise response activities
- preserve digital evidence
- support legal and regulatory obligations
- improve organizational resilience
- strengthen cyber defense
- support continuous learning
- improve operational readiness
- remain technology independent

Incident Response shall operate as a shared Enterprise capability.

---

# 2. Scope

This standard applies to all Enterprise incident response and digital forensics capabilities including

- incident detection
- incident verification
- incident classification
- incident containment
- incident eradication
- incident recovery
- digital forensics
- evidence preservation
- forensic investigations
- crisis coordination
- regulatory notification
- executive reporting
- post-incident reviews
- forensic readiness
- lessons learned

This standard applies regardless of deployment model or hosting provider.

---

# 3. Enterprise Incident Response Principles

Enterprise Incident Response shall be governed by the following principles.

## Rapid Response

Security incidents shall be identified, verified and contained as quickly as possible.

---

## Evidence Preservation

Digital evidence shall be protected throughout the incident lifecycle.

---

## Risk-Based Decision Making

Incident response activities shall prioritize business impact, operational risk and regulatory obligations.

---

## Standardized Procedures

All investigations shall follow documented Enterprise incident response procedures.

---

## Continuous Learning

Every security incident shall contribute to improving Enterprise security capabilities.

---

## Technology Independence

Enterprise Incident Response shall remain independent of specific incident response platforms, forensic tools and infrastructure vendors.

# 6. Enterprise Incident Response Architecture

The Enterprise Incident Response Architecture establishes the framework for managing cybersecurity incidents throughout their entire lifecycle.

The architecture consists of

- Incident Detection Services
- Incident Response Coordination
- Incident Management Services
- Digital Forensics Services
- Evidence Management Services
- Crisis Management Integration
- Recovery Coordination
- Communication Services
- Reporting Services
- Governance Services

The architecture shall ensure standardized and coordinated incident response across the Enterprise.

# 7. Incident Lifecycle

Every Enterprise security incident shall follow a standardized lifecycle.

The lifecycle shall include

- preparation
- detection
- verification
- classification
- containment
- eradication
- recovery
- post-incident review
- lessons learned
- continuous improvement

Each lifecycle phase shall be documented, measurable and auditable.

---

# 8. Incident Detection

Enterprise Incident Response depends upon rapid and accurate incident detection.

Detection sources shall include

- SIEM alerts
- SOC investigations
- endpoint detection
- cloud monitoring
- identity monitoring
- vulnerability monitoring
- user reports
- automated threat intelligence
- third-party notifications
- regulatory notifications

Incident detection shall integrate with Enterprise Security Monitoring defined in EA-365.

---

# 9. Incident Verification

Every detected event shall undergo verification before being declared an incident.

Verification activities shall evaluate

- event authenticity
- attack indicators
- affected assets
- affected identities
- business impact
- operational impact
- evidence quality
- confidence level
- regulatory implications
- escalation requirements

Verification decisions shall be documented and retained.

---

# 10. Incident Classification

Enterprise incidents shall be classified using standardized categories.

Classification shall include

- malware
- ransomware
- phishing
- credential compromise
- insider threat
- unauthorized access
- denial of service
- supply chain compromise
- data breach
- cloud security incident

Classification shall remain consistent throughout the Enterprise.

---

# 11. Incident Severity Levels

Incident severity shall be determined using standardized Enterprise criteria.

Severity evaluation shall consider

- business criticality
- operational disruption
- financial impact
- legal implications
- regulatory obligations
- customer impact
- data sensitivity
- recovery complexity
- exploit sophistication
- Enterprise reputation

Severity levels shall guide escalation, communication and response priorities.

---

# 12. Incident Containment

Incident containment shall minimize the impact of active security incidents.

Containment strategies may include

- endpoint isolation
- account suspension
- privileged access restriction
- network segmentation
- firewall rule updates
- workload quarantine
- cloud resource isolation
- API blocking
- service shutdown
- emergency configuration changes

Containment actions shall preserve evidence whenever practical.

---

# 13. Incident Eradication

Following containment, the Enterprise shall remove the root cause of the incident.

Eradication activities may include

- malware removal
- credential reset
- vulnerability remediation
- configuration correction
- infrastructure rebuilding
- unauthorized account removal
- persistence elimination
- software updates
- key rotation
- secrets replacement

Eradication activities shall be validated before recovery begins.

---

# 14. Incident Recovery

Recovery activities shall restore affected services while minimizing operational risk.

Recovery shall include

- service restoration
- integrity validation
- configuration verification
- monitoring enhancement
- business validation
- customer communication
- resilience verification
- security validation
- operational approval
- post-recovery monitoring

Recovery shall only be considered complete after validation confirms that the incident has been successfully resolved.

---

Enterprise Incident Response depends upon

- Enterprise Security Monitoring & SIEM
- Enterprise Security Operations Center

- Enterprise Infrastructure Services

- manually maintained evidence records

- unmanaged communication channels

The Enterprise Incident Response Architecture shall remain centralized, interoperable and governed across the Enterprise.

# 16. Enterprise Digital Forensics Architecture

The Enterprise Digital Forensics Architecture establishes the framework for preserving, acquiring, analyzing and presenting digital evidence throughout the incident response lifecycle.

The architecture consists of

- Evidence Collection Services
- Chain of Custody Management
- Forensic Acquisition Services
- Evidence Preservation Services
- Forensic Analysis Services
- Malware Analysis Services
- Timeline Analysis Services
- Reporting Services
- Legal Coordination Services
- Governance Services

Digital Forensics shall operate as an integrated capability within Enterprise Incident Response.

---

# 17. Evidence Collection

Digital evidence shall be collected using standardized Enterprise procedures.

Evidence collection shall support

- endpoint acquisition
- server acquisition
- cloud evidence collection
- container evidence collection
- Kubernetes evidence collection
- API transaction capture
- authentication records
- audit trails
- network captures
- security telemetry

Evidence collection shall preserve integrity and authenticity throughout the collection process.

---

# 18. Chain of Custody

Every evidence item shall maintain a complete Chain of Custody.

Chain of Custody documentation shall include

- evidence identifier
- collection time
- collection method
- collector identity
- evidence location
- transfer history
- storage history
- access history
- integrity verification
- disposal record

Every transfer of evidence shall be documented and auditable.

---

# 19. Evidence Preservation

Digital evidence shall remain protected against alteration throughout its lifecycle.

Evidence preservation shall include

- cryptographic hashing
- integrity verification
- immutable storage
- secure access control
- encrypted storage
- redundant storage
- retention management
- audit logging
- legal preservation
- secure destruction

Evidence integrity shall be verified before every forensic analysis activity.

---

# 20. Forensic Imaging

Where appropriate, forensic imaging shall be performed before investigative activities begin.

Forensic imaging shall support

- disk acquisition
- virtual machine snapshots
- cloud volume snapshots
- removable media imaging
- mobile device acquisition
- offline analysis
- integrity verification
- image validation
- evidence preservation
- repeatable analysis

Original evidence shall remain preserved while analysis is performed on verified forensic copies.

---

# 21. Memory Acquisition

Volatile memory acquisition shall be performed whenever relevant to the investigation.

Memory acquisition may support

- malware detection
- credential recovery
- encryption key analysis
- process analysis
- network connection analysis
- injected code detection
- runtime artifacts
- persistence analysis
- exploit reconstruction
- forensic correlation

Memory acquisition shall occur before shutdown whenever practical.

---

# 22. Log Preservation

Enterprise logging shall be preserved immediately following incident confirmation.

Log preservation shall include

- operating system logs
- application logs
- audit logs
- authentication logs
- SIEM events
- cloud audit trails
- Kubernetes audit logs
- API logs
- firewall logs
- network telemetry

Preserved logs shall remain protected against modification.

---

# 23. Timeline Analysis

Timeline analysis shall reconstruct the sequence of incident events.

Timeline analysis shall correlate

- authentication events
- process execution
- network activity
- configuration changes
- privilege changes
- file modifications
- cloud events
- API activity
- administrative actions
- security alerts

Timeline reconstruction shall support accurate incident investigation and root cause determination.

---

# 24. Malware Analysis

Where malware is involved, forensic investigations shall include malware analysis.

Malware analysis may include

- static analysis
- dynamic analysis
- behavioral analysis
- persistence analysis
- command-and-control analysis
- payload analysis
- capability assessment
- indicator extraction
- attribution support
- detection improvement

Malware analysis results shall improve Enterprise detection engineering and defensive capabilities.

Every significant incident shall include a documented Root Cause Analysis.

Root Cause Analysis shall determine

- attack vector
- exploited vulnerability
- control failures
- process deficiencies
- configuration weaknesses
- identity weaknesses
- monitoring effectiveness
- response effectiveness
- business impact
- preventive improvements

Root Cause Analysis shall drive corrective and preventive actions across the Enterprise.

---

# 30. Enterprise Incident Response Anti-Patterns

The following architectural anti-patterns are prohibited.

## Delayed Incident Escalation

Confirmed incidents shall never be intentionally delayed before escalation.

---

## Uncontrolled Evidence Handling

Evidence shall never be collected, modified or transferred outside approved Chain of Custody procedures.

---

## Incomplete Documentation

Every significant response activity shall be documented and retained.

---

## Premature System Restoration

Affected systems shall not return to production before containment, eradication and validation have been completed.

---

## Uncoordinated Communications

Incident communications shall always follow approved Enterprise communication procedures.

---

## Missing Lessons Learned

Every significant incident shall conclude with documented lessons learned and improvement actions.
