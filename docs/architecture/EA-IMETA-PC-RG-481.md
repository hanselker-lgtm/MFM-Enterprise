# 2. Architectural Transition

RG-481 deliberately opens a new architectural family.

RG-469–475 established predictive and adaptive transformation control.

RG-476–480 established multi-agent autonomy, resilience, zero-trust security, security operations and converged defence.

RG-481 therefore does **not** create another security or autonomy variation. It changes the primary architectural question:

> **How should an enterprise autonomously understand scarce resources, economic value, opportunity cost, capacity, constraints and strategic priorities, and allocate resources dynamically while preserving governance, fairness, resilience and human authority?**

The architecture moves from:

```text
CONTROL OF AUTONOMY
        ↓
TO
AUTONOMOUS CONTROL OF ENTERPRISE ECONOMICS
```

This is the beginning of a distinct architectural family.

# 3. Purpose

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-481 establishes a governed enterprise economic intelligence and resource-allocation layer.

The model applies to financial capital, people capacity, technology capacity, operational capacity, time, attention, inventory, infrastructure, data capacity, energy, supplier capacity and other scarce enterprise resources.

The architecture SHALL distinguish:

```text
RESOURCE
≠
CAPACITY
≠
COST
≠
VALUE
≠
BENEFIT
≠
OUTCOME
≠
OPPORTUNITY COST
```

It SHALL support autonomous recommendations and bounded autonomous allocation without allowing optimisation to override enterprise governance, statutory constraints, strategic commitments or protected human decisions.

# 4. Core Principle

> **Every material resource allocation SHALL be explainable in terms of value, constraints, opportunity cost, uncertainty, risk, strategic priority and expected outcome; autonomous optimisation SHALL operate within explicit economic and governance boundaries.**

# 164. Acceptance Criteria

EA-IMETA-PC-RG-481 is accepted when:

- enterprise resources and capacity are represented as governed economic objects;
- resource ownership, availability, commitment and reservation are reconstructable;
- demand and supply are forecast with confidence;
- scarcity and bottlenecks are visible;
- value, marginal value, marginal cost and opportunity cost are considered;
- strategic priorities and protected capacity are explicit;
- allocation floors, ceilings and envelopes exist;
- dynamic and adaptive allocation are governed;
- economic agents have explicit mandates and authority;
- economic conflicts and dissent are visible and arbitrated;
- fairness and allocation bias can be assessed where applicable;
- resource starvation, over-allocation, hoarding and waste can be detected;
- economic provenance and explainability are preserved;
- scenario, sensitivity and stress economics are supported;
- rebalancing is controlled against excessive churn;
- security, resilience and transformation guardrails remain authoritative;
- AI cannot alter strategic priorities or protected economic constraints;
- material allocation remains auditable and appropriately human-governed;
- negative and scenario tests prevent unsafe autonomous resource allocation.
