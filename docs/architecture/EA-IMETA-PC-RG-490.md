# 2. Architectural Position

RG-489 established future-state convergence, option economics, decision-window orchestration and adaptive commitment.

RG-490 extends this into the **network level**. The enterprise is no longer treated as a collection of independent commitments. Capital, resources, capacity, technology, suppliers, programmes and strategic initiatives form a connected commitment graph in which one commitment can enable, constrain, delay or invalidate another.

```text
RG-488 FORESIGHT
        ↓
RG-489 ADAPTIVE COMMITMENT
        ↓
RG-490 COMMITMENT NETWORKS
        ↓
CROSS-PORTFOLIO CLEARING
        ↓
EXECUTION CONVERGENCE
        ↓
SYNCHRONISED ADAPTIVE EXECUTION
        ↓
AUTONOMIC COMMITMENT GOVERNANCE
```

# 3. Core Architectural Question

**How can the enterprise coordinate thousands of interdependent commitments across portfolios, capital, resources, capacity, programmes and strategic objectives without creating hidden contention, uncontrolled commitment cascades or autonomous authority beyond the approved governance envelope?**

RG-490 therefore introduces commitment networks, commitment clearing, dependency synchronisation, cross-portfolio arbitration and execution convergence as first-class architecture capabilities.

# 4. Governing Principle

**No material commitment SHALL be evaluated solely in isolation when it consumes, enables, constrains or depends upon shared enterprise capacity. Enterprise commitment SHALL be cleared across material dependencies before execution, and autonomous systems SHALL coordinate only within explicitly authorised commitment corridors and governance boundaries.**

```text
STRATEGY
   ↓
COMMITMENT NETWORK
   ↓
DEPENDENCY GRAPH
   ↓
CROSS-PORTFOLIO CLEARING
   ↓
AUTHORITY GATE
   ↓
EXECUTION CONVERGENCE
   ↓
ADAPTIVE EXECUTION
   ↓
RECONCILIATION
```

# 7. Commitment Network Model

The enterprise commitment architecture SHALL represent commitments as a directed graph.

```text
             CAPITAL
               ↓
STRATEGY → COMMITMENT A → CAPACITY
               ↓             ↓
          COMMITMENT B → RESOURCE
               ↓
          COMMITMENT C
```

Each node SHALL expose ownership, authority, value, criticality, timing and state. Each edge SHALL identify the relationship and its operational significance.

# 8. Commitment Edge Types

Minimum relationship types SHALL include:

| Edge | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ENABLES | Source makes target possible |
| REQUIRES | Target requires source |
| CONSTRAINS | Source limits target |
| PRECEDES | Source must occur first |
| COMPETES | Both consume scarce shared capacity |
| SUPPORTS | Source improves target execution |
| CASCADES | Source creates downstream commitments |
| SUBSTITUTES | One path can replace another |
| CONFLICTS | Commitments cannot coexist within current constraints |
| AMPLIFIES | Source increases target exposure |

Cross-portfolio clearing SHALL resolve material claims before execution.

```text
PORTFOLIO A ─┐

PORTFOLIO C ─┤
PORTFOLIO D ─┘
```

Clearing SHALL consider strategy, criticality, timing, value, resilience, liquidity, resource scarcity and alternative execution paths.

```text
TOTAL CAPACITY
− RESERVED CAPACITY
− CRITICAL CAPACITY
− ACTIVE COMMITMENTS
− BUFFER
```

Headroom SHALL not be treated as freely allocatable when resilience or strategic reserves apply.

# 14. Capital and Liquidity Clearing

Capital clearing SHALL coordinate commitments against capital mandates.

Liquidity clearing SHALL protect:

- minimum liquidity;
- strategic reserves;
- recovery reserves;
- resilience reserves;
- committed obligations;
- known future windows.

A high-value commitment SHALL be blocked when it creates an unauthorised liquidity breach.

# 15. Resource Clearing

Resource clearing SHALL account for:

- scarcity;
- substitutability;
- lead time;
- quality;
- strategic reservation;
- common dependencies;
- geographic or supplier concentration.

Local optimisation SHALL not silently consume enterprise-critical scarce resources.

# 16. Strategic Clearing

Strategic clearing ensures that competing commitments remain aligned with authorised strategic priorities.

If two commitments are economically attractive but strategically incompatible, the conflict SHALL be escalated to the appropriate human authority.

- resource assumptions change;
- scope changes;
- authority changes;
- strategic conditions change.

Material divergence SHALL trigger re-evaluation rather than silent continuation.

# 25. Adaptive Execution

Adaptive execution MAY change sequencing, resource allocation, timing and implementation details within an approved corridor.

It SHALL NOT change strategic intent, hard constraints or authority boundaries.

Deadlock SHALL be detected where two or more commitments mutually block progress.

Resolution MAY include:

2. resource substitution;

4. decomposition;

# 31. Commitment Amplification

A small primary commitment can create disproportionate downstream exposure.

The architecture SHALL therefore calculate or estimate:

```text
PRIMARY EXPOSURE
+
DOWNSTREAM EXPOSURE
+
SECOND-ORDER EXPOSURE
```

# 32. Commitment Substitution

Where a commitment path fails, substitution SHALL identify alternatives that preserve strategic intent with lower network exposure.

Substitution SHALL respect authority, security, resilience and resource constraints.

# 33. Commitment Decomposition

Large commitments SHOULD be decomposed where this improves:

- reversibility;
- clearing;
- accountability;
- risk isolation;
- adaptive execution.

Decomposition SHALL not create artificial fragmentation that hides total exposure.

# 34. Commitment Aggregation

Compatible commitments MAY be aggregated where common execution creates value.

Aggregation SHALL preserve:

- ownership;
- auditability;
- individual authority;
- failure isolation;
- strategic traceability.
