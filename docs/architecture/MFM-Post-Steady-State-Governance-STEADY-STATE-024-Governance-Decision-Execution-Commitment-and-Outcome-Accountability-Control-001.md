# 1. Purpose

STEADY-STATE-024 establishes the controlled mechanism that connects an authorized governance decision to explicit commitment, accountable execution, delivery ownership, acceptance criteria and outcome accountability.

The purpose is to prevent a formally approved decision from becoming an ambiguous or ownerless implementation activity.

The operating sequence is:

```text
AUTHORIZED DECISION
↓
COMMITMENT
↓
ACCOUNTABILITY
↓
EXECUTION PLAN
↓
RESOURCE / CAPACITY CONFIRMATION
↓
IMPLEMENTATION
↓
PROGRESS CONTROL
↓
DELIVERY / ACCEPTANCE
↓
OUTCOME HANDOVER
↓
ACCOUNTABILITY REVIEW
```

---

# 2. Core Principle

Authorization creates permission to act; it does not by itself create execution accountability.

The following distinctions are mandatory:

```text
DECISION
≠
COMMITMENT

COMMITMENT
≠
ACTION

ACTION
≠
COMPLETION

COMPLETION
≠
OUTCOME

DELIVERY
≠
VALUE REALIZATION

OWNER
≠
AUTHORITY

RESPONSIBILITY
≠
ACCOUNTABILITY

PLAN
≠
ACTUAL EXECUTION
```

---

---

# 4. Decision-to-Execution Boundary

The transition from decision to execution shall be explicit:

```text
DECISION
↓
AUTHORITY CONFIRMED
↓
COMMITMENT CREATED
↓
OWNER ASSIGNED
↓
EXECUTION AUTHORIZED
↓
WORK COMMENCES
```

No material execution activity should rely solely on an informal assumption that a decision has been approved.

---

# 5. Commitment

A commitment is a governed obligation to deliver an agreed result, action or condition within defined boundaries.

A material commitment should identify:

```text
Commitment ID
Decision ID
Deliverable
Owner
Authority
Due Date
Dependencies
Resources
Acceptance Criteria
Status
```

---

# 6. Commitment Boundary

A commitment shall not be considered complete merely because:

```text
WORK STARTED
```

Completion requires defined acceptance or closure criteria.

---

# 7. Accountability

Accountability identifies who is answerable for the commitment and its result.

A material commitment should identify:

```text
Accountable Owner
Responsible Executor
Approving Authority
Supporting Roles
```

Where these roles differ, the distinction shall remain explicit.

---

# 8. Responsibility vs Accountability

The framework distinguishes:

```text
RESPONSIBILITY
=
WHO PERFORMS THE WORK

ACCOUNTABILITY
=
WHO IS ANSWERABLE FOR THE RESULT
```

One person may hold both roles, but they are not conceptually identical.

---

# 9. Delegation

Where execution responsibility is delegated:

```text
ORIGINAL ACCOUNTABILITY
↓
DELEGATED RESPONSIBILITY
```

Delegation does not automatically transfer ultimate accountability unless the governance model explicitly permits it.

```text

≠
```

# 23. Acceptance Authority

Acceptance should be performed by an appropriately authorized role.


```text

↓
ASSESS
↓

↓
REDECIDE IF NECESSARY
```

STEADY-STATE-014 applies where the issue becomes a formal exception or escalation.

---

# 30. Commitment Closure

A material commitment may be administratively closed when:

```text
Deliverable Completed
AND
Acceptance Completed
AND
Evidence Captured
AND
Outstanding Conditions Recorded
AND
Owner Confirmed Closure
```

Administrative closure does not automatically mean benefit or value has been realized.

---

# 31. Outcome Handover

```text
DELIVERY
↓
OPERATIONAL HANDOVER
↓
OUTCOME OWNER
↓
MEASUREMENT
↓
VALUE / BENEFIT REALIZATION
```

The handover shall be explicit where accountability changes.

---

# 32. Outcome Ownership

A material initiative should identify:

```text
Delivery Owner

Benefit Owner Where Applicable
Accountable Authority
```

This prevents the common failure where a project closes but no one owns the resulting outcome.
