# 1. Purpose

This document defines the ninety-eighth implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-97 – Enterprise Risk, Compliance, Internal Control, Audit, Policy Governance & Regulatory Assurance Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish a controlled third-party, supplier, procurement, contract, vendor risk, service provider and supply-chain assurance capability covering procurement governance, supplier lifecycle, vendor lifecycle, third-party governance, contract governance, supplier onboarding, due diligence, third-party risk assessment, supplier classification, critical supplier management, service provider management, contract obligations, SLA / OLA alignment, supplier performance, supplier incidents, supplier security, supplier privacy, subcontractor governance, supply-chain risk, contract renewal and termination, and supplier assurance.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must ensure that third parties are selected, assessed, contracted, onboarded, monitored, governed and offboarded according to business need, risk, compliance, security, privacy, service and financial requirements throughout the supplier lifecycle.**

# 125. Definition of Ready

A third-party work item is Ready when:

- The business requirement is defined.
- Supplier or service scope is understood.
- Budget and authority are established.
- Criticality and risk are assessed or ready for assessment.
- Security, privacy, compliance and continuity requirements are identified.
- Contract and performance requirements are known.
- Ownership is assigned.

# 127. Final Procurement Principle

> **Procurement decisions must be authorized, transparent, proportionate to risk and aligned with business value and applicable requirements.**

---

# 128. Final Due Diligence Principle

> **Material suppliers must be assessed before commitment in proportion to their criticality, access, data, dependency and risk.**

---

# 129. Final Contract Principle

> **Material supplier obligations must be explicit, assigned, stored in an authoritative repository and monitored throughout the contract lifecycle.**

---

# 130. Final Performance Principle

> **Supplier performance must be measured against defined service, quality, cost, risk and compliance expectations.**

---

# 131. Final Security Principle

> **Third parties with access to MFM systems, services or information must meet appropriate security requirements and remain subject to proportionate assurance.**

---

# 132. Final Privacy Principle

> **Third parties processing personal data must operate within defined privacy, security, processing, retention and deletion requirements.**

---

# 133. Final Continuity Principle

> **Critical supplier dependencies must be understood and supported by appropriate continuity, recovery and exit arrangements.**

---

# 134. Final Subcontractor Principle

> **Material subcontractors must be visible and governed so that outsourcing does not create uncontrolled downstream risk.**

---

# 135. Final Exit Principle

> **Supplier termination must include controlled transition, access revocation, data handling, asset return, financial closure and evidence.**

---

# 136. Final Assurance Principle

> **Third-party assurance must provide evidence-based confidence that suppliers continue to meet contractual, operational, security, privacy, compliance and resilience requirements.**

---

# 137. Final Integration Principle

> **Third-Party Governance must integrate with Procurement, Finance, Legal, Risk, Security, Privacy, Service Management, Identity, Records, Architecture, Business Continuity and Enterprise Assurance.**

---

# 138. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should manage third parties through a controlled lifecycle connecting procurement, due diligence, risk assessment, contracting, onboarding, performance, security, privacy, continuity, assurance, renewal and exit.**
