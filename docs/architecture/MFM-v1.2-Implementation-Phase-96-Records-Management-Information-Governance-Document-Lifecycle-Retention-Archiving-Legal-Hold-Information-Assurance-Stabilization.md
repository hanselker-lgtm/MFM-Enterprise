# 1. Purpose

This document defines the ninety-sixth implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-95 – Privacy, Data Protection, Information Rights, Consent, Records Compliance & Privacy Assurance Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish a controlled records and information governance capability covering records management governance, information governance, document lifecycle, records classification, metadata governance, filing and indexing, retention schedules, legal hold, disposition, archiving, records access, records integrity, records authenticity, information search, information retrieval, version control, document disposition, information lifecycle and records assurance.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must ensure that organizational information and records remain identifiable, authentic, reliable, accessible, appropriately protected, retained for defined periods and disposed of in a controlled and demonstrable manner.**

# 132. Definition of Ready

A records or information governance work item is Ready when:

- The information or record category is identified.
- The business purpose and owner are defined.
- Classification requirements are understood.
- Metadata requirements are identified.
- Retention and disposition requirements are identified.
- Legal hold and privacy considerations are understood.
- Access and security requirements are known.

# 134. Final Record Principle

> **Information that serves as evidence of organizational activity must be captured and maintained as an authoritative record when required.**

---

# 135. Final Authenticity Principle

> **Records must remain sufficiently authentic, reliable and attributable to support their intended evidentiary and operational purpose.**

---

# 136. Final Integrity Principle

> **Records must be protected against unauthorized alteration, loss or destruction throughout their required lifecycle.**

---

# 137. Final Metadata Principle

> **Metadata is part of information governance and must remain sufficiently complete and accurate to support identification, search, access, retention and assurance.**

---

# 138. Final Retention Principle

> **Records must be retained according to defined business, legal, regulatory and governance requirements and not disposed of prematurely.**

---

# 139. Final Legal Hold Principle

> **Valid legal, regulatory or investigative holds must suspend conflicting disposition activities until the hold is formally released.**

---

# 140. Final Disposition Principle

> **Disposition must be authorized, controlled, evidenced and verified rather than treated as an informal deletion activity.**

---

# 141. Final Archive Principle

> **Information requiring long-term preservation must be archived in a manner that maintains authenticity, integrity, context, accessibility and discoverability.**

---

# 142. Final Search Principle

> **Authorized users must be able to locate and retrieve required records without compromising privacy, security, legal hold or access controls.**

---

# 143. Final Lifecycle Principle

> **Information governance must manage information from creation or receipt through classification, use, retention, preservation and final disposition.**

---

# 144. Final Assurance Principle

> **Records assurance must provide evidence-based confidence that information and records are captured, governed, protected, retrievable and disposed of according to approved requirements.**

---

# 145. Final Integration Principle

> **Records and Information Governance must integrate with Privacy, Security, Identity, Data Governance, Architecture, Service Management, Legal, Risk, Compliance, Document Management and Enterprise Assurance governance.**

---

# 146. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should manage information through a controlled lifecycle connecting document creation, record capture, classification, metadata, access, retention, legal hold, archiving, disposition, preservation and continuous assurance.**
