# 1. Purpose

This document defines the one-hundred-and-ninth implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-108 – Financial Governance, Accounting Controls, Budgeting, Procurement, Cost Management, Financial Reporting & Fiscal Assurance Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish a controlled third-party governance capability covering supplier governance, vendor lifecycle, contract lifecycle, supplier onboarding, due diligence, third-party risk, supplier classification, procurement-to-contract integration, contract obligations, supplier performance, SLA / OLA supplier dependencies, supplier incidents, supplier changes, supplier renewals, supplier termination, concentration risk, supply-chain security, third-party compliance and supplier assurance.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must govern third parties throughout their lifecycle so that suppliers and service providers are appropriately selected, risk-assessed, contracted, monitored, measured, controlled, renewed or terminated with clear accountability and evidence.**

# 141. Definition of Ready

A third-party governance work item is Ready when:

- Supplier, vendor, service provider or contract context is identified.
- Business and contract ownership are established.
- Criticality, risk, data access and service dependencies are understood.
- Due diligence, security, privacy, compliance and financial requirements are identified.
- Required evidence, approvals and assurance activities are defined.

---

        ↓

# 143. Final Supplier Principle

> **Every material supplier must have clear ownership, appropriate classification, risk assessment, due diligence and lifecycle governance.**

---

# 144. Final Contract Principle

> **Material supplier commitments must be governed through controlled contracts with explicit obligations, ownership, performance expectations and exit provisions.**

---

# 145. Final Third-Party Risk Principle

> **Third-party risk must be assessed according to criticality, dependency, information exposure, security, compliance, financial and resilience factors.**

---

# 146. Final Performance Principle

> **Supplier performance must be measured against contractual and service expectations and acted upon when performance falls below agreed requirements.**

---

# 147. Final Security Principle

> **Supplier access and third-party technology dependencies must be protected through least privilege, appropriate security requirements, monitoring and lifecycle controls.**

---

# 148. Final Exit Principle

> **Critical supplier relationships must remain operationally exit-ready so that MFM can recover control of services, information, assets and access when required.**

---

# 149. Final Assurance Principle

> **Supplier assurance must provide evidence-based confidence that material third-party obligations, controls and risks are appropriately governed.**

---

# 150. Final Integration Principle

> **Third-Party Governance must integrate with Financial, Procurement, Contract, Security, Privacy, Service, Risk, Compliance, Project, Grant, Data, Configuration and Enterprise Architecture governance.**

---

# 151. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should govern suppliers through a controlled lifecycle connecting sourcing, due diligence, contracting, onboarding, risk, service levels, performance, incidents, changes, renewals, termination and assurance.**
