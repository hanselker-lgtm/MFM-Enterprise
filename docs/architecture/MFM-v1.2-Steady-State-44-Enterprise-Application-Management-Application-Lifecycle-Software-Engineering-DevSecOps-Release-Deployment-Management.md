# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the forty-fourth document in the MFM v1.2 Steady-State series.

It follows MFM v1.2-Steady-State-43 – Enterprise Service Management, ITSM, Service Desk, Incident, Problem, Change, Request, Knowledge & Service Level Management.

The purpose of this document is to establish the permanent enterprise operating model for application management, application portfolio governance, software lifecycle management, software engineering, application architecture, secure software development, DevSecOps, source-code management, requirements engineering, testing, quality assurance, release management, deployment management, environment management, application configuration, dependency management, application observability, application support, technical debt, application resilience, application security, application data management, application continuity, application suppliers, application retirement and continual application improvement.

The central objective is:

> **MFM applications must be governed and engineered throughout their lifecycle so that software remains secure, maintainable, reliable, supportable, compliant, performant and aligned with business outcomes.**

The primary objective is:

> **Ensure that applications remain aligned with business requirements, technically sustainable, secure, supportable and appropriately governed throughout their lifecycle.**

# 126. Definition of Ready

An application, requirement, software component, defect, vulnerability, release, deployment, recovery activity, technical-debt item, finding, exception or improvement work item is Ready when:

- The application need, requirement, component, defect, vulnerability, release, deployment, recovery activity, technical debt, finding, exception or improvement is clearly identified.
- Ownership, criticality, dependencies, security, privacy, data, service, continuity, architecture and business requirements are known.
- Design, implementation, testing, approval, deployment, rollback, evidence, validation and acceptance requirements are defined.

# 128. Final Application Governance Principle

> **Applications must be governed as enterprise assets with clear ownership, lifecycle accountability, business alignment and measurable technical health.**

---

# 129. Final Engineering Principle

> **Software must be engineered through controlled, repeatable and testable practices that produce secure, reliable and maintainable outcomes.**

---

# 130. Final DevSecOps Principle

> **Security and operational requirements must be integrated into software delivery rather than treated as activities separate from development.**

---

# 131. Final Release Principle

> **Releases must be planned, tested, risk-assessed, authorized and validated before being considered complete.**

---

# 132. Final Deployment Principle

> **Production deployments must be repeatable, observable, recoverable and appropriately controlled.**

---

# 133. Final Security Principle

> **Application security must address architecture, code, dependencies, identity, access, data, configuration, testing and operational monitoring throughout the lifecycle.**

---

# 134. Final Technical Debt Principle

> **Technical debt must be visible, risk-assessed, owned and progressively reduced where it materially affects security, resilience, maintainability, cost or business capability.**

---

# 135. Final Lifecycle Principle

> **Applications must remain actively governed from acquisition or creation through operation, improvement and controlled retirement.**

---

# 136. Final Assurance Principle

> **Material application controls and engineering practices must be supported by evidence and periodically assessed for effectiveness.**

---

# 137. Final Improvement Principle

> **Incidents, defects, vulnerabilities, technical debt, user feedback, releases, operational data, audits and engineering lessons must continuously improve MFM application capability.**

---

# 138. Final Integration Principle

> **Application Management must integrate with Enterprise Architecture, Service Management, Infrastructure, Data Management, Cybersecurity, Privacy, Risk, Business Continuity, Financial Management, Procurement, Supplier Management, People Management, Compliance, Legal and Project Management.**

---

# 139. Final Steady-State Application Principle

> **MFM applications must be governed and engineered throughout their lifecycle so that software remains secure, maintainable, reliable, supportable, compliant, performant and aligned with business outcomes.**
