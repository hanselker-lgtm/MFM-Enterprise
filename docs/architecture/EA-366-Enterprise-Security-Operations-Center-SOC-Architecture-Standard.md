# Architecture Compliance

This standard defines the Enterprise Security Operations Center (SOC) Architecture for the MFM Enterprise Platform.

General Enterprise Architecture principles are inherited from EA-020.

Enterprise Architecture positioning is inherited from EA-111.

Enterprise Event Architecture principles are inherited from EA-112.

Infrastructure Architecture principles are inherited from EA-320.

Integration Architecture principles are inherited from EA-340 through EA-345.

Enterprise Information Architecture principles are inherited from EA-350 through EA-359.

Enterprise Security Architecture principles are inherited from EA-360.

Enterprise Identity & Access Management Architecture principles are inherited from EA-361.

Enterprise Zero Trust Architecture principles are inherited from EA-362.

Enterprise Cryptography & PKI Architecture principles are inherited from EA-363.

Enterprise Secrets Management Architecture principles are inherited from EA-364.

Enterprise Security Monitoring & SIEM Architecture principles are inherited from EA-365.

All Enterprise SOC implementations shall comply with this standard.

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this standard is to establish the Enterprise Security Operations Center Architecture governing operational security monitoring, incident handling, threat hunting, security orchestration and operational governance across the MFM Enterprise Platform.

The Enterprise SOC shall

- provide continuous security operations
- coordinate incident response
- improve detection effectiveness
- reduce response times
- strengthen cyber resilience
- support Zero Trust Architecture
- enable automated security operations
- support regulatory compliance
- improve operational visibility
- remain technology independent

The SOC shall operate as a shared Enterprise capability.

---

# 2. Scope

This standard applies to all Enterprise SOC capabilities including

- Security Operations Center
- incident triage
- incident management
- threat hunting
- threat intelligence operations
- security orchestration
- security automation
- case management
- digital evidence handling
- operational dashboards
- analyst workflows
- playbooks
- SOC reporting
- operational governance
- continuous improvement

This standard applies regardless of deployment model or hosting provider.

---

# 3. Enterprise SOC Principles

Enterprise Security Operations shall be governed by the following principles.

## Continuous Operations

Security operations shall continuously monitor, investigate and respond to security events.

---

## Risk-Based Prioritization

Security operations shall prioritize activities according to Enterprise risk, business impact and threat intelligence.

---

## Automation First

Operational processes shall be automated wherever practical.

---

## Human Oversight

Automated decisions with significant operational impact shall remain subject to human review where appropriate.

---

## Standardized Operations

Security investigations shall follow standardized Enterprise procedures and documented playbooks.

---

## Technology Independence

Enterprise SOC Architecture shall remain independent of specific vendors, SIEM platforms, SOAR platforms and cloud providers.

---

The Enterprise SOC Architecture shall

- reduce Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)

- improve investigation quality

# 10. Incident Triage

Every detected security event shall undergo structured triage.

Incident triage shall evaluate

- severity
- business impact
- affected assets
- affected identities
- confidence level
- threat indicators
- operational urgency
- regulatory impact
- customer impact
- escalation requirements

Triage decisions shall be documented and auditable.

Classification shall consider

- ransomware

- unauthorized access

- data breach
- supply chain compromise

Severity classifications shall remain consistent across the Enterprise.

- recovery complexity

# 24. Enterprise SOC Anti-Patterns

The following architectural anti-patterns are prohibited.

## Ad Hoc Incident Handling

Security incidents shall not be managed without documented procedures and standardized workflows.

---

## Manual-Only Operations

Manual investigations shall not replace Enterprise automation where repeatable operational processes can be automated safely.

---

## Missing Case Documentation

All investigations shall maintain complete documentation sufficient for audit, forensic review and operational learning.

---

## Uncontrolled Automation

Security automation shall never execute high-impact actions without governance, testing and appropriate approval mechanisms.

---

## Isolated Threat Intelligence

Threat intelligence shall not remain disconnected from detection engineering, monitoring and incident response.

---

## Undefined Operational Ownership

Every operational activity shall have clearly assigned ownership and accountability.
