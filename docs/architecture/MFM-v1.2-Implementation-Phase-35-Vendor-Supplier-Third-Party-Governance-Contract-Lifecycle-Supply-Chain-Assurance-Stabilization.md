# 1. Purpose

This document defines the thirty-fifth implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-34 – Enterprise Compliance Management, Regulatory Obligations, Policy Governance & Compliance Monitoring Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish the enterprise vendor, supplier, third-party governance, contract lifecycle and supply-chain assurance baseline for MFM.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must govern third-party relationships throughout their lifecycle, from identification and due diligence through onboarding, contracting, performance monitoring, risk management, incident handling and controlled exit, while preserving security, privacy, financial, compliance and operational assurance.**

# 134. Final Vendor Principle

> **Every material supplier relationship must have an accountable owner, defined purpose, known criticality and governed lifecycle.**

---

# 135. Final Due-Diligence Principle

> **Supplier due diligence must be proportionate to the potential business, security, privacy, financial and continuity impact of the relationship.**

---

# 136. Final Contract Principle

> **Material contractual obligations must be identifiable, owned, monitored and supported by evidence.**

---

# 137. Final Performance Principle

> **Supplier performance must be measured against agreed expectations and material failures must result in controlled corrective action.**

---

# 138. Final Security Principle

> **Third-party access must be explicitly authorized, limited, attributable, monitored and revocable according to risk.**

---

# 139. Final Privacy Principle

> **Third parties processing personal information must remain within governed privacy, security, retention and deletion requirements.**

---

# 140. Final Concentration Principle

> **Critical dependence on a single supplier, platform, region or technology must be visible and actively managed where material.**

---

# 141. Final Continuity Principle

> **Critical supplier relationships must have continuity expectations and, where practical, a credible alternative or exit strategy.**

---

# 142. Final Exit Principle

> **Supplier termination must protect MFM through controlled access revocation, data handling, knowledge transfer and evidence preservation.**

---

# 143. Final Assurance Principle

> **Third-party assurance must be evidence-based and proportionate to supplier criticality and residual risk.**

---

# 144. Final Lifecycle Principle

> **Third-party governance must continue after onboarding and remain active through operation, renewal, change, incident and exit.**

---

# 145. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should govern suppliers and third parties as extensions of its operational, security, privacy, compliance and resilience boundary, with explicit ownership, risk-based assurance and controlled lifecycle management.**
