# 1. Purpose

This document defines the thirty-seventh implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-36 – Enterprise Architecture Portfolio, Capability Management, Application Portfolio & Technology Lifecycle Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish the enterprise service-management, IT operations, service catalog, service-level and operational-performance baseline for MFM.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must manage business and technology services through defined ownership, service definitions, operational procedures, service levels, incident and request integration, availability and capacity management, continuity expectations, knowledge management and measurable service performance.**

# 125. Definition of Ready

A service-management work item is Ready when:

- Service or operational objective is identified.
- Consumer is known.
- Owner is assigned.
- Criticality is assessed.
- Dependencies are known.
- Support requirements are understood.
- SLA / availability / continuity needs are considered.
- Security, privacy and compliance impacts are assessed.

---

# 127. Final Service Principle

> **Every material service must have a clear owner, defined consumer, known purpose, lifecycle state and measurable service expectations.**

---

# 128. Final Catalog Principle

> **The service catalog must describe services as they actually operate, not merely as they were originally designed.**

---

# 129. Final SLA Principle

> **Service-level commitments must be measurable from reliable operational data and supported by defined breach handling.**

---

# 130. Final Availability Principle

> **Critical service availability must be measured consistently and recurring failures must drive corrective improvement.**

---

# 131. Final Capacity Principle

> **Capacity management must anticipate demand rather than wait for service degradation to become an incident.**

---

# 132. Final Continuity Principle

> **Critical services must have proportionate recovery expectations, dependency awareness and tested continuity arrangements.**

---

# 133. Final Operations Principle

> **A service is not operationally complete until ownership, support, monitoring, recovery, documentation and escalation are ready for real-world operation.**

---

# 134. Final Knowledge Principle

> **Operational knowledge must remain current, owned, searchable and actionable.**

---

# 135. Final Improvement Principle

> **Incidents, problems, service-level results, capacity constraints and user feedback must feed a measurable continual-improvement process.**

---

# 136. Final Lifecycle Principle

> **Service governance must continue from design through operation, improvement and retirement.**

---

# 137. Final Integration Principle

> **Services must connect operational management with incidents, problems, changes, architecture, vendors, data, security, privacy, risk and financial information where relevant.**

---

# 138. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should manage services as governed operational products with explicit ownership, measurable performance, controlled support, resilience expectations and continuous improvement.**
