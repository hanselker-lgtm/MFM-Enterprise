# 1. Purpose

This document defines the one-hundred-and-fourth implementation phase following MFM v1.2-Implementation-Phase-103 – Business Process Architecture, Process Governance, BPM, Process Automation, Case Management, Workflow Control & Process Assurance Stabilization.

The purpose of this phase is to establish a controlled service management capability covering service management governance, service portfolio, service catalog, service ownership, service lifecycle, service level management, SLA / OLA, service requests, request fulfilment, incident management, major incident management, problem management, root cause analysis, change enablement, release management, deployment governance, operational performance, service reporting, service monitoring and operational assurance.

The central objective is:

> **MFM must provide stable, measurable and accountable services with clearly defined ownership, service expectations, request and incident handling, problem resolution, controlled change and release practices, and evidence-based operational assurance.**

# 147. Definition of Ready

A service management work item is Ready when:

- Service or operational context is identified.
- Ownership is established.
- Business impact and consumer needs are understood.
- Relevant SLA, support, security, privacy and continuity requirements are identified.
- Dependencies and affected configuration items are known.
- Testing, monitoring and assurance requirements are understood.

# 149. Final Service Principle

> **Every material service must have clear ownership, defined outcomes, support arrangements, dependencies and measurable service expectations.**

---

# 150. Final Catalog Principle

> **Consumers should be able to discover approved services and understand what they provide, how they are supported and how they can be requested.**

---

# 151. Final SLA Principle

> **Service-level commitments must be measurable, reportable and actively managed when performance falls below agreed expectations.**

---

# 152. Final Incident Principle

> **Incident management must restore service quickly while preserving sufficient information for communication, analysis and learning.**

---

# 153. Final Problem Principle

> **Recurring or material incidents must drive structured problem analysis and corrective action to reduce recurrence.**

---

# 154. Final Change Principle

> **Changes must be assessed and authorized according to risk, implemented through controlled procedures and validated after implementation.**

---

# 155. Final Release Principle

> **Releases must be operationally ready, tested, supportable, monitored and recoverable before introduction into live service.**

---

# 156. Final Monitoring Principle

> **Critical services must be sufficiently observable to detect degradation, support diagnosis and enable timely operational response.**

---

# 157. Final Assurance Principle

> **Service assurance must provide evidence-based confidence that services meet defined performance, security, continuity, support and governance requirements.**

---

# 158. Final Integration Principle

> **Service Management must integrate with Enterprise Architecture, Business Process, Data, Integration, Security, Privacy, Configuration, Change, Resilience, Supplier, Financial and Enterprise Assurance governance.**

---

# 159. Final Implementation Principle

> **MFM should manage services through a controlled lifecycle connecting service design, catalog, ownership, service levels, requests, incidents, problems, changes, releases, monitoring, performance and continuous improvement.**
