# Architecture Compliance

This standard defines the Enterprise Application Portfolio Management Architecture for the MFM Enterprise Platform.

General Enterprise Architecture principles are inherited from EA-020.

Enterprise Architecture positioning is inherited from EA-111.

Enterprise Event Architecture principles are inherited from EA-112.

Infrastructure Architecture principles are inherited from EA-320.

Integration Architecture principles are inherited from EA-340 through EA-345.

Enterprise Information Architecture principles are inherited from EA-350 through EA-359.

Enterprise Security Architecture principles are inherited from EA-360 through EA-369.

Enterprise Application Architecture principles are inherited from EA-400.

All Enterprise Application Portfolio activities shall comply with this standard.

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this standard is to establish a consistent Enterprise framework for governing the complete application portfolio throughout its lifecycle.

The Enterprise Application Portfolio Management capability shall

- align applications with business strategy
- optimize application investments
- reduce technical debt
- eliminate redundant applications
- improve architectural consistency
- support modernization initiatives
- improve governance
- strengthen compliance
- enable portfolio transparency
- remain technology independent

Enterprise Application Portfolio Management shall function as an Enterprise-wide governance capability.

---

# 2. Scope

This standard applies to

- internally developed applications
- commercial off-the-shelf applications
- SaaS applications
- cloud-native applications
- legacy applications
- mobile applications
- web applications
- desktop applications
- shared enterprise platforms
- business-critical applications

The standard applies regardless of ownership, hosting model or deployment technology.

---

# 3. Enterprise Application Portfolio Principles

Enterprise Application Portfolio Management shall be governed by the following principles.

## Business Value First

Applications shall demonstrate measurable business value.

---

## One Capability – One Primary Application

Each major business capability should preferably be supported by one primary application.

---

## Lifecycle Governance

Every application shall have a defined lifecycle.

---

## Architectural Transparency

Application ownership, dependencies and lifecycle status shall always be documented.

---

## Continuous Rationalization

The portfolio shall continuously eliminate redundancy and obsolete applications.

---

## Technology Independence

Portfolio decisions shall support long-term architectural flexibility.

---

The Enterprise Application Portfolio shall

- improve business alignment

# 6. Enterprise Application Inventory

The Enterprise shall maintain a complete and continuously updated inventory of all applications.

The application inventory shall include

- application identifier
- application name
- business owner
- technical owner
- business domain
- supported business capabilities
- deployment model
- hosting platform
- technology stack
- lifecycle status
- vendor information
- licensing information
- support model
- security classification
- criticality classification
- compliance status
- dependencies
- recovery objectives
- integration landscape
- architectural documentation
- retirement target where applicable

The application inventory shall be regarded as the authoritative Enterprise source for application governance.

# 7. Business Capability Mapping

Every Enterprise application shall be mapped to one or more approved business capabilities.

Business capability mapping shall

- identify application purpose
- identify supported business processes
- identify capability ownership
- identify application overlap
- identify capability gaps
- identify modernization opportunities
- identify integration requirements
- identify dependency chains
- support investment planning
- support portfolio optimization

Business capabilities shall remain independent from organizational structures.

# 24. Enterprise Application Portfolio Anti-Patterns

The following portfolio management anti-patterns are prohibited.

## Duplicate Applications

Multiple applications providing the same primary business capability shall be avoided unless justified by approved Enterprise Architecture decisions.

---

## Unknown Ownership

Applications shall always have documented business and technical owners.

---

## Unsupported Applications

Applications without vendor or internal support shall not remain in production beyond approved exception periods.

---

## Permanent Legacy Systems

Legacy applications shall have documented modernization or retirement strategies.

---

## Uncontrolled SaaS Adoption

Business units shall not procure SaaS solutions outside Enterprise governance.

---

## Technology Proliferation

Technology diversity shall be managed to reduce operational complexity and improve maintainability.
