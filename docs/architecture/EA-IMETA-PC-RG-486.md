# 2. Architectural Position

RG-485 established enterprise economic equilibrium across capital, liquidity, resources, capacity, value, scarcity and markets.

RG-486 addresses conditions where ordinary rebalancing is insufficient because the environment changes materially or a disturbance propagates through the enterprise.

```text
RG-481 ECONOMIC DECISION
        ↓
RG-482 RESOURCE INTELLIGENCE
        ↓
RG-483 VALUE NETWORK
        ↓
RG-484 CAPITAL DYNAMICS
        ↓
RG-485 ECONOMIC EQUILIBRIUM
        ↓
RG-486 SYSTEMIC ECONOMIC ADAPTATION
```

The central question is:

> How can the enterprise detect regime change, contain systemic shocks, preserve critical economic functions, recover through governed trajectories and adapt its economic configuration without allowing autonomy to rewrite strategy or constitutional governance?

# 3. Core Principle

**The enterprise SHALL absorb economic shocks, contain propagation, stabilise critical functions, recover through governed trajectories and adapt to persistent regime change while preserving strategy, security, resilience, liquidity, authority and constitutional constraints.**

```text
OBSERVE → DETECT → CLASSIFY → PROPAGATE → CONTAIN
        → STABILISE → RECOVER → ADAPT → RE-EQUILIBRATE
        → VERIFY → LEARN
```

# 4. Architectural Shift from RG-485

RG-485 asks: **Where is the enterprise out of balance?**

RG-486 adds: **What caused the imbalance, how will it propagate, is the operating regime changing, and how should the enterprise adapt?**

This creates:

```text
EQUILIBRIUM
   ↓
SHOCK
   ↓
PROPAGATION
   ↓
CONTAINMENT
   ↓
RECOVERY
   ↓
ADAPTATION
   ↓
NEW EQUILIBRIUM
```

This is deliberately different from adding more optimisation. Some conditions require systemic adaptation rather than better local optimisation.

# 8. Shock Propagation Architecture

```text
SHOCK SOURCE
   ↓

   ↓
DEPENDENCY GRAPH
   ↓
SECOND-ORDER EFFECT
   ↓
THIRD-ORDER EFFECT
   ↓

   ↓

```

Propagation analysis SHALL identify source, affected nodes, path, latency, amplification, damping, containment points, criticality and confidence. Observed propagation SHALL be distinguished from modelled propagation.

# 9. Shock Classification

Shocks SHALL be classified by source, magnitude, duration, latency, persistence, correlation, propagation, reversibility, uncertainty and systemic criticality.

A small local shock MAY be more important than a large isolated shock when it crosses a highly central dependency.

# 10. Compound and Cascading Shocks

A compound shock contains multiple overlapping disturbances. A cascading shock occurs when one disturbance triggers further disturbances.

```text
SHOCK
 ↓
LIQUIDITY PRESSURE
 ↓
CAPITAL CONSTRAINT
 ↓
RESOURCE UNDERFUNDING
 ↓
CAPACITY LOSS
 ↓
VALUE LOSS
 ↓
SECONDARY LIQUIDITY PRESSURE
 ↺
```

Feedback loops SHALL be identified because they can turn temporary disruption into systemic instability.

# 11. Economic Regime Architecture

The architecture SHALL distinguish normal, stressed, recovery, adaptive and transformation regimes.

Regime classification SHALL use multiple indicators and confidence. A regime transition requires evidence that material economic relationships or constraints have changed.

# 15. Adaptive Architecture

Adaptation SHALL be classified as incremental, configurational, structural or transformational.

The least disruptive sustainable adaptation SHOULD be preferred. Repeated failure of incremental adaptation SHALL trigger consideration of structural adaptation.

# 16. Continuous Re-equilibration

```text

      → OBSERVE RESULT → UPDATE STATE → repeat
```

The loop SHALL include damping, minimum evidence requirements and authority checks to prevent autonomous churn.

```text

   ↑

       /
      /
     /

```

Alternative trajectories SHOULD be compared rather than assuming a single recovery path.

# 20. Adaptation Windows

The architecture SHALL distinguish:

```text
TIME TO REACT
TIME TO ADAPT
TIME TO RECOVER
TIME TO TRANSFORM
```

Adaptation windows SHALL consider adaptation cost, future risk, recovery cost, optionality and reversibility.

# 21. Shock Containment

Containment priority SHALL generally be:

1. critical economic functions;
2. liquidity integrity;
3. security and resilience constraints;
4. critical resource capacity;
5. high-centrality dependencies;
6. strategic value streams;
7. discretionary activity.

Containment SHALL be assessed for secondary effects.

# 22. Feedback and Stability

The architecture SHALL monitor:

```text
POSITIVE FEEDBACK → AMPLIFICATION
NEGATIVE FEEDBACK → STABILISATION
DELAYED FEEDBACK → OSCILLATION
NON-LINEAR FEEDBACK → TIPPING RISK
```

Adaptive controls SHALL use damping when response latency makes over-correction likely.

# 23. Resilience and Optionality

Resilience means ability to absorb, adapt and recover. Optionality means preserving future choices.

A resilient system can still have low optionality if it is locked into one configuration. RG-486 SHALL therefore preserve both when economically justified.

# 24. Economic Memory and Learning

Learning SHALL retain:

```text
SHOCK → RESPONSE → ASSUMPTIONS → ACTUAL EFFECT
      → RECOVERY → SIDE EFFECTS → LESSON → CONFIDENCE
```

Learning evidence SHALL not automatically become policy. Policy changes require the appropriate governance path.
