# MFM v1.2-Steady-State-51
## Enterprise Business Continuity, Crisis Management, Disaster Recovery, Resilience & Operational Recovery

**Version:** 1.2  
**Document ID:** MFM-v1.2-Steady-State-51  
**Status:** Steady-State Enterprise Continuity, Resilience & Recovery Governance Baseline  
**Lifecycle:** Post-Implementation / Steady-State Operation

---

# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the fifty-first document in the MFM v1.2 Steady-State series.

It follows MFM v1.2-Steady-State-50 – Enterprise Risk Management, Compliance, Legal, Audit, Assurance & Regulatory Governance.

The purpose is to establish the permanent enterprise operating model for business continuity, operational resilience, crisis management, disaster recovery, technology recovery, service recovery, data recovery, emergency response, business impact analysis, continuity strategies, recovery objectives, recovery plans, crisis communications, dependency management, resilience testing, recovery exercises, backup and restoration, alternate operating arrangements, critical supplier continuity, emergency governance, post-incident recovery, continuity assurance, resilience metrics, recovery maturity and continual resilience improvement.

> **MFM must remain capable of protecting critical people, services, information, assets and obligations during disruption and must restore prioritized operations within defined recovery requirements through tested, governed and continuously improved continuity and recovery capabilities.**

# 2. Scope

Business continuity; operational resilience; crisis management; emergency management; disaster recovery; IT and technology recovery; application, infrastructure, network, cloud and data recovery; backup and restore; workforce and facility continuity; supplier and third-party continuity; business impact analysis; critical services and processes; dependencies; RTO; RPO; maximum tolerable downtime; maximum tolerable data loss; impact tolerance; recovery priorities; continuity strategies; minimum service levels; degraded mode; manual workarounds; alternate sites; remote working; crisis activation; crisis leadership; emergency decision rights; crisis teams; communications; stakeholder notification; recovery plans; BCPs; DRPs; crisis plans; runbooks; recovery validation; failover; failback; continuity and recovery exercises; tabletop and simulation exercises; technical recovery tests; supplier recovery tests; lessons learned; recovery resources and capacity; recovery security and data protection; continuity assurance; findings; exceptions; remediation; resilience and recovery metrics; dashboards; maturity and continual improvement.

# 3. Business Continuity Objective

Maintain critical business services at defined minimum levels during disruption and restore normal operations according to approved priorities.

# 4. Operational Resilience Objective

Ensure critical services can withstand, adapt to, respond to and recover from disruption within approved impact tolerances.

# 5. Crisis Management Objective

Provide coordinated leadership, decision-making, communication and resource mobilization during material disruptions and crises.

# 6. Disaster Recovery Objective

Restore critical technology, applications, infrastructure, networks and data within approved recovery objectives.

# 7. Recovery Objective

Recover people, processes, technology, facilities, suppliers, information and services in controlled sequence according to business priorities and dependencies.

# 8. Continuity Principles

Continuity should be business-led, risk-based, prioritized, tested, documented, recoverable, resilient, proportionate and continuously improved.

# 9. Resilience Principles

Resilience should provide the capability to prevent, withstand, respond, adapt, recover and learn.

# 10. Crisis Principles

Crisis management should be fast, coordinated, authorized, evidence-based, communications-ready and decision-oriented.

# 11. Recovery Principles

Recovery should be prioritized, sequenced, controlled, tested, secure, validated and documented.

# 12. Continuity Lifecycle

Identify → Analyze → Plan → Prepare → Exercise → Respond → Recover → Improve.

# 13. Continuity Governance

The Continuity Governance Authority coordinates business continuity, operational resilience, crisis management, disaster recovery, recovery planning, testing and assurance.

# 14. Continuity Roles

Relevant roles may include Continuity Authority, Business Continuity Manager, Operational Resilience Owner, Crisis Manager, Crisis Leader, Business Recovery Owner, IT Disaster Recovery Owner, Technology Recovery Owner, Application Recovery Owner, Data Recovery Owner, Facilities Continuity Owner, Workforce Continuity Owner, Supplier Continuity Owner and Communications Lead.

# 15. Continuity Ownership

Each critical service, process, dependency and recovery capability should have accountable ownership.

# 16. Critical Services

Critical services should be identified according to business, customer, regulatory, financial, operational, reputation and safety impact where applicable.

# 17. Critical Processes

Critical processes should be linked to required people, technology, data, facilities and suppliers.

# 18. Business Impact Analysis

BIA should determine critical services, critical processes, impact, priorities, dependencies and recovery requirements.

# 19. Impact Categories

Assess people, operations, customers, members, finance, legal, regulatory, security, privacy and reputation impacts as applicable.

# 20. Maximum Tolerable Downtime

Material critical services should have defined maximum tolerable downtime where appropriate.

# 21. Recovery Time Objective

RTO defines the target time within which a service or capability should be restored after disruption.

# 22. Recovery Point Objective

RPO defines acceptable data loss measured in time where applicable.

# 23. Maximum Tolerable Data Loss

Where appropriate, maximum tolerable data loss should be defined independently from RPO.

# 24. Impact Tolerance

Critical services should have approved impact tolerances where an operational-resilience model is used.

# 25. Recovery Priority

Priorities should consider business criticality, dependencies, RTO, RPO, impact tolerance and resource availability.

# 26. Dependency Mapping

Critical services should map people, processes, technology, applications, infrastructure, networks, data, facilities, suppliers and third parties.

# 27. Recovery Sequencing

Recovery should follow approved sequencing based on dependencies and business priorities.

# 28. Continuity Strategy

Strategies may include redundancy, resilience, alternate sites, remote working, manual workaround, supplier substitution, capacity reduction and degraded service.

# 29. Minimum Service Level

Critical services should define the minimum acceptable service level during disruption.

# 30. Degraded Mode

Where appropriate, services should support controlled degraded operation.

# 31. Manual Workarounds

Critical processes should identify manual alternatives where automated processing is unavailable.

# 32. Alternate Sites

Alternate operating locations should be identified where required.

# 33. Alternate Facilities

# 34. Remote Working Continuity

Secure remote-working arrangements should support continuity where appropriate.

# 35. Workforce Continuity

# 36. Key Person Dependency

# 37. Workforce Substitution

# 38. Crisis Management

Crisis management coordinates assessment, decision, escalation, communication, resource allocation and recovery.

# 39. Crisis Activation

Activation criteria should define when enhanced governance is required.

# 40. Crisis Levels

# 41. Crisis Leadership

# 43. Emergency Decision Rights

# 44. Crisis Communications

# 46. Emergency Notifications

Emergency notification mechanisms should support timely critical instructions.

# 47. Contact Trees

# 48. Incident Escalation

# 49. Disaster Recovery

# 50. IT Disaster Recovery

# 53. Infrastructure Recovery

# 55. Cloud Recovery

# 63. Disaster Recovery Plans

# 66. Runbooks

# 67. Recovery Procedures

# 68. Recovery Validation

# 69. Failover

# 70. Failback

# 73. Third-Party Continuity

# 74. Supplier Recovery Testing

# 76. Regulatory Notifications

# 77. Legal Notifications

# 82. Technical Recovery Tests

# 83. Failover Tests

# 84. Failback Tests

Failback tests should verify safe return to primary operating arrangements.

# 85. Crisis Exercises

Crisis exercises should test leadership, decision rights, communications, stakeholder management and recovery.

# 86. Exercise Evidence

# 87. Lessons Learned

Material exercises and disruptions should generate lessons and improvement actions.

# 88. Recovery Incident Management

# 90. Recovery Prioritization

# 91. Recovery Resource Management

# 92. Recovery Capacity

# 93. Recovery Security

# 94. Recovery Data Protection

Recovered data must retain appropriate privacy and security protection.

# 95. Recovery Documentation

# 96. Recovery Evidence

Recovery actions should create sufficient evidence for decision, execution, validation, reporting and assurance.

# 97. Continuity Assurance

Continuity assurance provides confidence that critical recovery capabilities remain effective.

# 98. Resilience Assurance

Resilience assurance assesses whether critical services remain within approved impact tolerances.

# 128. Definition of Ready

A critical service, BIA, continuity plan, crisis plan, recovery plan, recovery test, exercise, backup arrangement, supplier continuity activity, finding, exception or improvement work item is Ready when the requirement is clear; ownership, criticality, impact, RTO, RPO, impact tolerance, dependencies, resources and obligations are known; and strategy, procedure, testing, communication, validation, evidence and acceptance requirements are defined.

# 129. Definition of Done

A continuity or recovery work item is Done when:

```text
Service / Scenario / Requirement Identified
        ↓
Owner / Team Assigned
        ↓
BIA / Strategy / Plan / Recovery Action Completed
        ↓
Testing / Exercise / Validation Completed
        ↓
Documentation Updated
        ↓
Evidence Captured
        ↓
Findings / Exceptions Addressed
        ↓
Business Acceptance Completed
```
